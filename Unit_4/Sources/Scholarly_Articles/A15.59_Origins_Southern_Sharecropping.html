RALPH SHLOMOWITZ THE ORIGINS OF SOUTHERN SHARECROPPING During the past few years there has been an outpouring of literature on the economic history of the postbellum American South and important contributions have been made to our understanding of the institutional structure of the postbellum rural economy and to the nexus between this institutional structure and the performance of the postbellum rural economy.' One of the main focuses of this recent work has been the study of labor arrangements between planters and the emancipated black workers, usually called freedmen, and, more explicitly, the inves- tigation of the causes and consequences of the labor arrangement called sharecropping.2 However, despite this not inconsiderable research effort, the mechanism of the transition to the system of sharecropping remains obscure. That such an important gap in our knowledge about the post- bellum South exists has been recognized by a number of prominent scholars. The paper extends the evidential base on which to study this subject by using an important, but largely un- tapped, unpublished primary source, namely, the monthly and annual reports and the labor contracts of the Bureau of Refugees, Freedmen, and Abandoned Lands, usually referred to as the Freedmen's Bureau.4 The Freedmen's Bureau was created by Congress in 1865 to supervise the disposition of abandoned or confiscated lands and property, to issue rations, clothes, and medicine to destitute refugees and freedmen, and to approve and to enforce labor contracts. The task of supervising labor contracts lasted from 1865 to 1868 and during this period the Bureau assisted in the negotiation of several hundred thousand labor contracts. The structure of the paper will be as follows. In the second section, use is made of the monthly and annual reports of the officials of the Bureau to discuss the working of the contract labor sys,tem. In the third section, the sample of labor contracts is analyzed. In the fourth section, the mechanism of transition to the sharecropping system is analyzed. Finally, in the fifth section of this paper, the broader significance of this issue will be discussed.
