Reconstructing Andrew Johnson: The Influence of Laissez-Faire Constitutionalism on President Johnson's Restoration Policy
William E. Hardy

JOURNAL Tennessee Historical Quarterly 65, no. 1 (2006)
EXCERPT Original journal pages 71-72
JSTOR https://www.jstor.org/stable/42628583
ESSAY PROMPTS 15.1, 15.2
ARGUMENT ROLES 15.1.3 Supports; 15.2.2 Qualifies
Selection Overview

Hardy argues that Andrew Johnson's fidelity to laissez-faire constitutionalism and Jacksonian principles, including opposition to class legislation, significantly contributed to his breach with the Republican Congress. Treating race, class, region, and Jacksonianism together, he traces laissez-faire constitutionalism back to the founders, especially James Madison's Federalist no. 10, as a principle hostile to class-based legislation.
