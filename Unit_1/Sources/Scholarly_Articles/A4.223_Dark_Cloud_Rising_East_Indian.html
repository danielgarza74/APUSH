Selection Overview

Pulsipher argues that English refusal to honor the treaty promises that ended King Philip’s War led directly to King William’s War, and that the Wabanaki fought to defend their land and sovereignty, then used negotiations with both the French and the English to press for respect.

"Dark Cloud Rising from the East": Indian Sovereignty and the Coming of King William's War in New England JENNY HALE PULSIPHER IN 1676, Boston's leading minister, Increase Mather, pub lished an account of King Philip's War: its rise, its providential role in prodding New England to repentance, its battles and ambushes, and the capture and killing of its chief actor, Philip, or Metacom, of the Wampanoags. While Philip's death ended hostilities in southern New England, peace eluded Maine and New Hampshire. Referring to those troubled regions, Mather wrote, "And there seems to be a dark Cloud rising from the East in respect of Indians in those parts, yea a Cloud which streameth forth blood."' A treaty concluded the conflict in 1678, but after ten years of tenuous calm in the northeast, King William's War erupted.
