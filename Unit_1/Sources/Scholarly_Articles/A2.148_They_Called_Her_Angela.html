Selection Overview

Stevenson recovers the life of Angela, a young Central African woman landed in Virginia in 1619, to place Black women's experience at the center of the colony's slave society. She stresses that Angela's life under her master William Peirce was shaped by forced labor and likely sexual trauma, with limited social engagement beyond her labor and her Angolan female community.

Stevenson Early in 1619, slave traders captured and sold Central Africans, probably Kim- bundu speakers from the royal district of Ndongo, in Luanda, the capital of Portugal’s western central African colony. Baptized, renamed, and forced aboard the Portuguese São João Bautista, owned by Diogo Rodrigues of Lisbon and captained by Manuel Mendes da Cunha, 1 a small number of these persons disembarked that summer on the southern coast of the Virginia Peninsula. A female youth, called Angela by her captors, was among them.
