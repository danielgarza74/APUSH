Selection Overview

Bernhard argues that the familiar picture of seventeenth-century Virginia as a society of profit-driven planters must be set beside evidence of a community that did not treat poverty as a sin and made provision for its destitute members. Comparing Virginia with Massachusetts, she concludes that poverty was accepted as part of the natural order and relieved by planters who saw themselves as English country gentlemen.

The Virginia Magazine There is yet another paradox: Puritans, for all their religious rhetoric, were uncomfortable when confronted by poverty; but Virginians, who had not expected to encounter it, took poverty for granted. For the Puritans, poverty was an ideological dilemma which created strains in their holy community; but for the Virginians, poverty was a practical problem whose solution helped to build a social order and reinforced the colonists' image of themselves as transplanted English gentry.4 The early Virginians, conditioned by promotional literature and exposed for the first time to what appeared to be unlimited economic opportunities, came to the New World expecting to get rich. They did not assume, as did the Puritans who settled in Massachusetts, that in the New World "some must be rich, some poore."5 On the contrary, the promotional tracts for Virginia and the fantastic tobacco profits at first promised that "as for the generall sort that shall goe to bee planters, bee they never so poore, so they be honest, and painefull, the place will make them rich."6 Among the enticements offered by the tract writers in the days of the Virginia Company's campaign to attract settlers, the most pervasive theme was the cure of poverty. Persons living in "extreame poverty" in England, it was said, could possess a "hansome house of some foure roomes or more ... and twelve English Acres of ground, adjoyning" in Virginia.7 In the New World at last would be found a haven for the growing numbers of poor dislocated by enclosures of farmlands and rising prices in seventeenth- century England.
