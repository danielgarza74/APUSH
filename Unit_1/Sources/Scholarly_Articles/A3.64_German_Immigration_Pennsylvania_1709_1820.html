Selection Overview

Grubb asks what the social makeup of German immigrants can tell us about why they emigrated and how they fit into American society, noting that earlier evidence was fragmentary. He finds that roughly half of the immigrants entered servitude on arrival, and that many Germans did so as intact families, unlike British immigrants.

Or did they have an individualistic spirit, an independent migratory propens- ity, an extensive interest in education, books, and newspapers, many craft skills, and an ability quickly to assimilate Anglo- American culture?2 The literary and quantitative evidence on the social compo- sition of German immigrants has been fragmentary and less than representative. Literary evidence has many biases. For example, British colonists may have held prejudicial views of German im- migrants. Henry Melchior Muhlenberg, the head of the German Lutheran Church in Pennsylvania during the mid-eighteenth cen- tury, concluded that "The 'superior' nations look upon the Ger- mans as nothing but wig-blocks, which are thick and hard to be sure, but wanting brains." The quantitative evidence has been limited and has typically measured the character of German im- migrants either before they left their home village or after they had acclimatized to the New World, rather than at the point of debarkation in America.
