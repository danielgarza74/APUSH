Selection Overview

Thompson reopens what he calls the "Locke wars" over whether John Locke influenced Jefferson and the Declaration of Independence, aiming to close the debate with new evidence. Rather than tracing a genealogy of ideas, he examines how Jefferson's early-nineteenth-century contemporaries understood Locke's influence, and he sets his view against scholars who have downgraded Locke in favor of republican, common-law, radical Whig, and Scottish sources.

Its aim is to shed new light with new evidence on this rather time- worn controversy. In particular, I examine the sources and purposes of the Declaration from an entirely new perspective, that is, from the perspective of Jefferson’s post-1800 contemporaries. Rather than viewing Locke’s ideas from the front end, that is, by eluci- dating the genealogy of ideas extending from Locke (and others) to Jefferson and the Dec- laration, this article examines Locke’s inﬂuence on Jefferson and the Declaration from the back end, that is, by examining how early nineteenth-century Americans viewed Locke’s inﬂuence on Jefferson and the writing of the Declaration. It should matter, after all, how Jefferson’s fellow Americans understood what the Declaration was all about. John Locke’s inﬂuence on American revolutionary thought, once considered hegemonic (see Becker 1922; Webking 1988; Dworetz 1989; Pangle 1990; Huy- ler 1995; Zuckert 1996; Thompson 1998, 2019; Ward 2004), has been seriously questioned in recent decades. From Gordon S. Wood (1969) and J. G. A. Pocock (1975) to Barry Shain (1994) and John Phillip Reid (2003), Locke’s role in shap- ing the American mind has been downgraded to a supporting if not minor role. Nay, more: historians and political theorists over the past 40 years have expended great time and energy in search of a non-Lockean source to explain the intellectual causes of the American Revolution. In the quarter century between 1800 and 1825 an extraordinary but forgot- ten debate erupted in the United States over the nature, sources, authorship, originality, and purposes of the Declaration of Independence. Originally, the controversy erupted between the Federalist partisans of John Adams and the Republican partisans of Thomas Jefferson, but over time it turned into a deeply personal and sometimes petty feud between some of America’s most distin- guished individuals and families over the originality of Jefferson’s contribution to the Declaration. On a deeper level, however, it was a debate over the meaning and role of the Declaration of Independence in the life of the new nation. *** The presidential election of 1800 was one of the most bruising in American history. It was the ﬁrst election in the life of the young republic in which the nation divided ideologically between two warring factions within the broader revolutionary movement: the Federalists led by John Adams and the Republi- cans led by Thomas Jefferson.
