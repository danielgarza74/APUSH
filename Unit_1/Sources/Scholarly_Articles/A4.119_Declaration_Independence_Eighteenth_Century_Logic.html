Wilbur Samuel Howell

JOURNAL The William and Mary Quarterly 18, no. 4 (1961)
EXCERPT Original journal pages 463-464
JSTOR https://www.jstor.org/stable/1921097
Selection Overview

Howell starts from Jefferson's own statement that the Declaration aimed at no originality of principle but at the "common sense of the subject," and notes that Becker and Boyd read this as showing Jefferson drew on familiar, largely Lockean ideas. He then asks whether the Declaration also followed the accepted persuasive methods of its day, and argues that its argumentative structure matches the structure set out in the most significant eighteenth-century logic texts.
