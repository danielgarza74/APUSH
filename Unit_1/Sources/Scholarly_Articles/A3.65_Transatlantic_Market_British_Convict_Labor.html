Selection Overview

Grubb argues that the transportation of about 50,000 British convicts to America between 1718 and 1775, roughly a quarter of all British arrivals, was essentially an experiment in privatizing post-trial criminal justice. He builds a model of the trade and tests it against convict auction prices and shipper profits, concluding that the evidence supports the model.

The Transatlantic Market for British Convict Labor FARLEY GRUBB Convicts account for at least one-quarter ofBritish migration to mid-eighteenth-cen- tury America. Their transportation to and disposal in America was essentially an experiment in privatizing post-trial criminal justice. A model of this trade is devel- oped that yields testable implications regarding the relative distributional moments of convict auction prices, the size of shipper profits, and how convicts were selected for transportation. Quantitative evidence is assembled to test these implications and to measure the labor value of criminality. The results support the model and are used to explain the political economy of British penal policy. ". . . this Exporting of Felons to the Colonies ... implies retuirns.... MODELING THE CONVICT TRADE The Crown minimized the cost of overseas convict disposal by chann convicts through the existing transatlantic market for servant labor. features of the convict trade, therefore, were identical to those in the tured servant trade. Both indentured and convict servants received fixed- term alienable labor contracts before embarkation and their transportation was handled by the private sector for profit. Both were sold in America to private employers who bid the highest, with the monies received going to defray the shippers' transportation expenses. Many shippers carried both indentured and convict servants on the same voyage. While under contract in America indentured and convict servants were largely indistinguishable with regard to fulfillment of their contracts, the legal and customary rights and restrictions placed on their and their masters' behavior, the range of work performed, and the restoration of freedom upon contract completion. Important differences, however, existed between the trades.
