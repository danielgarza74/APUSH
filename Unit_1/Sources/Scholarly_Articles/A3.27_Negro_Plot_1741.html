The Negro Plot of 1741
T. Wood Clarke
 
ARTICLE The Negro Plot of 1741
JOURNAL New York History 25, no. 2 (1944)
EXCERPT Original journal pages 167-168
JSTOR https://www.jstor.org/stable/23147790
Selection Overview

Clarke compares New York City's 1741 "Negro Plot" to the Salem witchcraft prosecutions as an outbreak of mass hysteria, tracing how a burglary at Robert Hogg's house and the testimony of the young servant Mary Burton led to widespread arrests. He concludes that the supposed plot caused terrible suffering and many judicial murders, and that the fear it aroused made slavery unpopular in the city.
