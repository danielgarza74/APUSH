Primary Source P2.73
The Examination of Martha Corey
Salem magistrates and Martha Corey · March 21, 1692 (University of Virginia edition)
 
TOC FIT Chapter 2 · Salem · a church member accused
WHY HERE Notice how Martha Corey describes herself, how she answers the magistrates, and how they respond when she questions the girls’ fits. Ask what the magistrates treat as signs of guilt.
TREATMENT Public-domain text; modern transcription of a court record, excerpt
Historical Context

Martha Corey was a member of the church at Salem Village and the third wife of Giles Corey. She had expressed doubt about the girls’ accusations before she herself was named. On March 19, 1692, Edward Putnam and Henry Keney complained that she had harmed several girls, and she was brought before magistrates John Hathorne and Jonathan Corwin at Nathaniel Ingersoll’s house on March 21.

The examination was written down by the Rev. Samuel Parris. Several of the girls had fits during it, and the magistrates and the Rev. Nicholas Noyes of Salem Town questioned her about them. Martha Corey was held for trial and found guilty, and she was hanged on September 22, 1692.

Editorial note. The text is the University of Virginia’s transcription of SWP No. 38.2 from the manuscript in the Essex County Court Archives. Seventeenth-century spelling is kept, and the speakers are not always labeled, as in the original; “Mr H.” is Hathorne and “K.” is Corey. Only part of the long examination is reproduced.
