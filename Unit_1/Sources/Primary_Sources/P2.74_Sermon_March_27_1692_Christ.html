Sermon of March 27, 1692: “Christ Knows How Many Devils There Are in His Church”
Samuel Parris · March 27, 1692 (University of Virginia edition of the sermon notebook)
 
TOC FIT Chapter 2 · Salem · what ministers preached
WHY HERE Read how Parris describes the witchcraft outbreak and what he tells church members to do about it. Notice what he says about church membership and about who is in the church.
TREATMENT Public-domain text; modern transcription of a minister’s sermon notes, excerpt
Historical Context

Samuel Parris was the minister of the church in Salem Village, Massachusetts. His daughter Elizabeth (Betty) and his niece Abigail Williams were the first girls to be described as afflicted in early 1692. By March 1692 several women, including a member of his own church, had been questioned by magistrates and sent to jail on suspicion of witchcraft.

Parris preached this sermon on Sunday, March 27, 1692, which was a day for Communion (the Lord’s Supper). He took as his text John 6:70, “Have not I chosen you twelve, and one of you is a devil?” The document is his own notebook, written for preaching, and shows the outline and arguments he used rather than a word-for-word record of what the congregation heard.

Editorial note. The text is the University of Virginia’s transcription (by Benjamin Ray, verified against the 1993 edition by James F. Cooper Jr. and Kenneth P. Minkema) of Parris’s notebook, pages 147–151. Parris’s abbreviations (such as “&” and “wn”) are kept. The opening line is his own heading.
Document Text

Occasioned by dreadfull Witchcraft broke out here a few weeks past, & one Member of this Church, & another of Salem upon publick examination by Civil Authority vehemently suspected for Shee-Witches, & upon it Committed

[. . .]

4. Use Exhort in two branches.

1. To be deeply humbled for the appearances of Devils among our Churches. If the Church of Corinth were called to mourn because of one incestuous person among them. 1 Cor. 5 initio How much more may N-E Churches mourn that such as work witchcraft, or are vehemently suspected so to do should be found among them.

[. . .]

2. To be much in prayer that God would deliver our Churches from Devils. That God would not suffer Devils in the guise of Saints to associate with us. One sinner destroys much good; how much more one Devil. Pray we also that not one true Saint may suffer as a Devil, either in name, or body. The Devil would represent the best Saints as Devils if he could, but it is not easy to imagine that his power is of such extent, to the hazard of the Church.

[. . .]

5. Use. Last: Examine we our selves well, what we are: what we Church-members are: We are either Saints, or Devils, the scripture gives us no medium. The Apostle tells us we are to examine our selves. 2 Cor. 13. 5. Oh it is a dreadful thing to be a Devil, & yet to sit down at the Lords Table. 1 Cor. 10. 21. Such incurr the hottest of Gods wrath, as follows. 22. v. Now if we would not be Devils we must give our selves wholly up to Christ: & not suffer the predominancy of one lust, & particularly that lust of covetousness, which is made so light of, & which so sadly prevails in these perilous times: why this one lust made Judas a Devil. 12. Joh. 6. 26. Matth. 15. And no doubt it has made more Devils than one. For a little pelf, men sell Christ to his Enemies, & their Souls to the Devil. But there are certain sins that make us Devils, see that we be not such

1. A liar or plunderer. 8. Joh. 44.

2. A slanderer or an accuser of the godly

3. A tempter to sin

4. An opposer of godliness, as Elymas. 13. Acts. 8 &c.

5. Envious persons as Witches

6. A Drunkard 1. Sam. 1. 15. 16.

7. Last. A proud person.
