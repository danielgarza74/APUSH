All Slave-Keepers That Keep the Innocent in Bondage, Apostates (excerpt)
Benjamin Lay · 1737 (Wikisource edition)
 
TOC FIT Chapter 3 · A Quaker protest against slaveholding and the slave trade
WHY HERE Notice the comparison Lay draws between Christians’ treatment of enslaved Africans and what he says he learned about slavery under the Turks, and how he describes the buying and selling of captives in Africa.
TREATMENT Public-domain text, excerpt
Historical Context

Benjamin Lay (about 1682–1759) was an English-born Quaker who had worked as a sailor and lived in Barbados before settling near Philadelphia. He was an outspoken opponent of slavery, and his protests angered Quaker leaders who owned or traded enslaved people. His book was printed in Philadelphia in 1737 by Benjamin Franklin.

The book is a long, loosely organized mix of argument, scripture, and personal stories, written in an angry and intense style. Lay claimed to be reporting what he had seen and been told by sailors, so some of his statements are secondhand. The excerpt below shows two of his main themes: that slaveholders were hypocrites by their own religion, and that the trade itself was a business of kidnapping.

Editorial note. The text follows the Wikisource transcription of the 1737 Philadelphia printing. Lay’s capitalization, punctuation, and spelling are kept, including his use of “Negroe.” Biblical citations are as printed. The excerpt omits other sections of the long work.
Document Text

Well my Friends, consider of it, and make an Application suitable to the circumstance of your own Slaves; for I do not believe in my Soul, the Turks are so cruel to their Slaves, as many Christians, so called, are to theirs, by what I have seen and heard of, in Barbadoes; and elsewhere; and I give you a reason for it. I was near 18 Months; on board a large Vessel of 400 Tons in a Voyage to Scanderoon in Turky, with four Men that had been 17 Years Slaves in Turky, and I never did understand by them, that they were so badly used as the poor Negroes are by some called Christians.

[. . .]

But these Hellish Miscreants, these Men-Stealers, pretend they fetch away these poor Creatures, that they may not kill one the other, when they are the Murtherers which sets 'em to the Work, (a cursed work it is) for as I have had an Account, near 35 Years ago, when 30 or 12 Sail of Vessels come on the Coast of Guinea, and they cannot catch Negroes enough to Freight their Vessels by the Sea side, and in Rivers where they send their Boats in Search and Pursuit of them, where they are acquainted; for they being us'd to the Business know where to go; and to find out some old Negroes that they have been used to trade with, which will bring off in Canoes, their Wives or Children, or their Neighbours Wives, and Children if they can catch 'em in Woods, or any where else, so bring 'em and sell them to our brave Christians, which come there with Ships for that purpose.---O brave I give 30s. for a Negroe and fell him for 30l, or 40, 50 or 60, 70, 80, 90, 100l or more: Who would but be a Trader in Slaves and Souls of Men, altho' he goes to Hell for it, and in the mean time intail an Iniquity on his own, and his Neighbour's Posterity to their Destruction and the Ruin of the whole Country beside.
