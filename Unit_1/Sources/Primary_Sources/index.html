Reading these documents

Begin with the source itself.

Spelling, capitalization, and punctuation follow the reproduced edition. The long “s” of eighteenth-century printing has been modernized. The bracketed marker [. . .] identifies text omitted by the reader; unbracketed ellipses belong to the source or its editor. Editorial notes identify later printings, reconstructions, and memoirs written after the events.

Citing these documents

Cite the edition you quote.

Use the full footnote citation the first time and the shortened form afterward. Replace the model locator with the article, section, letter, entry date, or original page supporting your claim.

Reader page numbers are navigation aids, not original source pages. Include only sources you actually use in your bibliography.
