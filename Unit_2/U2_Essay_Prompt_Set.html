5.1 · What Really Explains the American Revolution?

Historians have long disagreed about what fundamentally drove the Revolution. Which interpretation does the evidence best support?

E Pluribus Unum · Unit 2 · Building a Nation

Historians have long disagreed about what fundamentally drove the Revolution. Which interpretation does the evidence best support?

Choose and defend one of the following positions (or stake out a qualified position between two):

  1. 5.1.1 Ideological/political: The Revolution was driven primarily by genuine ideological commitment to Whig political principles — fear of arbitrary power and a conviction that British policy threatened constitutional liberty.
  2. 5.1.2 Economic/class interest: The Revolution was driven primarily by material self-interest — colonial elites and merchants protecting property, trade, and land claims from British interference.
  3. 5.1.3 Social transformation from below: The Revolution was driven as much by internal colonial tensions — artisans, tenants, and “the lower sort” pushing for greater political and economic power — as by resistance to Britain.
  4. 5.1.4 Imperial/constitutional dispute: The Revolution grew out of an unresolved structural disagreement over sovereignty within the British Empire, not a rejection of British political values themselves.
  5. 5.1.5 Elite-led conservative movement: The Revolution was orchestrated partly by colonial elites to forestall more radical social change and preserve their own local authority against both Britain and popular unrest.
Keep in focus: Stay within 1763–1776. Name which interpretive tradition(s) you are engaging — do not simply list causes.
In Plain Terms

Main question: Why did the colonists actually rebel — was it about ideas, money, internal power struggles, or something else?

  • Option 1: They rebelled because they truly believed their rights and freedoms were under attack.
  • Option 2: Rich and powerful colonists rebelled mainly to protect their own money and land.
  • Option 3: Regular working people pushed the Revolution as much as it was a fight against Britain.
  • Option 4: It was really a legal argument about who was in charge that spiraled out of control.
  • Option 5: Colonial leaders started the Revolution partly to stay in control and stop bigger changes from happening.

5.2 · How Radical Was the American Revolution?

Did the Revolution fundamentally transform colonial society, or mainly replace British elites with American ones while leaving the existing order intact?

E Pluribus Unum · Unit 2 · Building a Nation

Did the Revolution fundamentally transform colonial society, or mainly replace British elites with American ones while leaving the existing order intact?

Choose and defend one of the following positions:

  1. 5.2.1 Genuinely radical: The Revolution dismantled deference and hierarchy, reshaping American social and political life.
  2. 5.2.2 Elite continuity: The same planter/merchant class retained control; little changed for ordinary people.
  3. 5.2.3 Uneven radicalism: The Revolution was radical for white men, but stagnant or worse for enslaved people, women, and Native Americans.
  4. 5.2.4 Political but not social radicalism: Government structures changed dramatically, but everyday social life and rank did not.
Keep in focus: Stay within 1776–1787. Specify which group(s) or dimension of society — political, economic, social — your argument covers.
In Plain Terms

Main question: Did the Revolution actually change how American society worked, or did the same people stay in charge?

  • Option 1: The Revolution really did flip society upside down.
  • Option 2: The rich stayed rich and in charge — not much changed for everyone else.
  • Option 3: Some people gained a lot of freedom; others gained little or lost ground.
  • Option 4: The system of government changed a lot, but daily life and social rank mostly didn’t.

5.3 · Whose Liberty? The Revolution and Slavery

Did the Revolution’s rhetoric of liberty meaningfully advance the position of enslaved and free Black Americans, or did it leave slavery more entrenched?

E Pluribus Unum · Unit 2 · Building a Nation

Did the Revolution’s rhetoric of liberty meaningfully advance the position of enslaved and free Black Americans, or did it leave slavery more entrenched?

Choose and defend one of the following positions:

  1. 5.3.1 Liberalizing momentum: The Revolution created real ideological pressure toward abolition, especially in the North.
  2. 5.3.2 Entrenchment: The Revolution hardened and justified slavery, especially in the South, by tying it to property rights.
  3. 5.3.3 Regionally contradictory: The Revolution’s effect on slavery was liberalizing in the North and entrenching in the South, with no single national outcome.
  4. 5.3.4 Centering enslaved agency: The more important story is what enslaved people did for their own freedom — for example, fleeing to British lines — not what white politicians promised.
Keep in focus: Stay within 1775–1787. Distinguish rhetoric from actual policy and legal outcomes.
In Plain Terms

Main question: Did the Revolution’s talk of “liberty” help enslaved people, or make things worse for them?

  • Option 1: The Revolution’s ideas about freedom actually helped get slavery ended in some places.
  • Option 2: The Revolution’s talk about property rights ended up protecting and strengthening slavery.
  • Option 3: It cut both ways depending on where you were — freer in the North, harsher in the South.
  • Option 4: The real story of liberty during the Revolution is what enslaved people did for themselves, not what leaders promised.
