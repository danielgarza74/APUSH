Primary Source P5.46
A Birch Certificate Issued to a Black Loyalist
Issued by order of Brigadier General Samuel Birch, British Army, New York · April 21, 1783
 
TOC FIT Chapter 5 · Essay prompt 5.3 (Whose Liberty? The Revolution and Slavery)
WHY HERE The certificate shows, in a single document, how British commanders’ wartime proclamations translated into passage out of the United States for Black Loyalists at the war’s end.
Historical Context

When the British evacuated New York in 1783, thousands of formerly enslaved people who had fled to British lines claimed the freedom promised by Sir William Howe’s and Sir Henry Clinton’s proclamations. Although the Treaty of Paris barred the British from carrying away American property, including enslaved people, Sir Guy Carleton refused to return those who had come in under the proclamations.

Brigadier General Samuel Birch, commandant of New York City, issued printed passes like this one, filled in by hand with the bearer’s name and the date. Those who left were then entered in a register of departures later called the “Book of Negroes,” which records about three thousand people who sailed for Nova Scotia and elsewhere between April and November 1783.

Editorial note: The unit proposal listed the “Book of Negroes” register itself (British copy, Sir Guy Carleton Papers, The National Archives, Kew, PRO 30/55/100; viewable through Nova Scotia Archives at https://archives.novascotia.ca/africanns/archives/?ID=26). Its digitized pages are published under Crown copyright, so this reader substitutes a related public-domain document created in the same evacuation process. The bearer’s name and the date are handwritten; the name is read here as Cato Ramsay. The image is cropped at the bottom in its source, so Birch’s signature line is incomplete. The owning repository is not identified in the image’s public record.
Document Text

NEW-YORK, 21st April 1783.

THIS is to certify to whomsoever it may concern, that the Bearer hereof Cato Ramsay a Negro, resorted to the British Lines, in consequence of the Proclamations of Sir William Howe, and Sir Henry Clinton, late Commanders in Chief in America; and that the said Negro has hereby his Excellency Sir Guy Carleton’s Permission to go to Nova-Scotia, or wherever else He may think proper.

By Order of Brigadier General Birch,
