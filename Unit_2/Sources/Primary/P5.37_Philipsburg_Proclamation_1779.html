Primary Source P5.37
The Philipsburg Proclamation
Sir Henry Clinton, British commander in chief in North America · June 30, 1779
 
TOC FIT Chapter 5 · Essay prompt 5.3 (Whose Liberty? The Revolution and Slavery)
WHY HERE This short military order sets out what the British army offered to enslaved people who reached its lines, and to whom that offer did not apply. Students can compare its terms with Dunmore’s 1775 proclamation and with Patriot laws and petitions, and can consider what it reveals about the choices open to enslaved people during the war.
Historical Context

Sir Henry Clinton had commanded the British army in North America since 1778. By mid-1779 he faced a stalemate around New York, the loss of troops to the Caribbean after France entered the war, and plans for a new campaign in the southern colonies. He issued this proclamation from his headquarters at Philipsburg, the confiscated Philipse family manor in Westchester County, New York, and it was printed in Loyalist newspapers.

Clinton’s order went further than Lord Dunmore’s 1775 proclamation, which had promised freedom only to enslaved men of rebel owners who would bear arms for the king. Clinton’s promise was not limited to soldiers or to one colony, but it still excluded people held by Loyalists. Tens of thousands of enslaved people fled to British lines during the war, although historians’ estimates vary widely. At the war’s end, British commanders refused to hand many of them back to their former owners.

Editorial note: Transcribed by the staff of Philipse Manor Hall State Historic Site from the proclamation as printed in the Royal Gazette (New York), July 4, 1779, according to the site’s caption. The transcription’s spelling and capitalization are preserved; its reading “all this Majesty’s Forces” in the heading appears to stand for “all his Majesty’s Forces.” The site’s introduction and commentary are omitted.
Document Text

By His Excellency Sir Henry Clinton, K. B. General and Commander in Chief of all this Majesty’s Forces, within the Colonies laying on the Atlantic Ocean, from Nova Scotia to West-Florida, inclusive, &c., &c., &c.

PROCLAMATION.

Whereas the Enemy have adopted a practice of enrolling Negroes among their Troops; I do hereby give Notice, That all Negroes taken in Arms, or upon any military Duty, shall be purchased for a stated Price; the Money to be paid to the Captors.

But I do most strictly forbid any Person to sell or claim Right over any Negroe, the Property of a Rebel, who may take Refuge with any Part of this Army; and I do promise to every Negroe Who shall desert the Rebel Standard, full Security to follow within these Lines; any Occupation which he shall think proper.

Given under my Hand at Head-Quarters, Philipsburgh, the 30th Day of June, 1779. H. Clinton.

By his Excellency’s Command, John Smith, Secretary.
