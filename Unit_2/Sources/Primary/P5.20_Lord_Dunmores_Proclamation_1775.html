Lord Dunmore’s Proclamation
John Murray, fourth Earl of Dunmore, royal governor of Virginia · November 7, 1775
 
TOC FIT Chapter 5 · Essay prompt 5.3 (Whose Liberty? The Revolution and Slavery)
WHY HERE The royal governor’s offer of freedom to servants and enslaved people of rebel owners who would fight for the king. Students can examine exactly whom the offer covered and whom it left out, what military and political purposes Dunmore gave for it, and how it framed the conflict for Virginia’s enslaved people and for their owners.
Historical Context

John Murray, the fourth Earl of Dunmore, became royal governor of Virginia in 1771. After he removed gunpowder from the public magazine at Williamsburg in April 1775 and armed resistance grew, he fled the capital in June and governed from ships off Norfolk, with only a small force of regular troops. He issued this proclamation aboard the ship William and made it public in mid-November, after his troops won a skirmish at Kemp’s Landing.

The proclamation declared martial law. It also promised freedom to indentured servants and enslaved people belonging to rebels, if they were able and willing to bear arms for the king. Hundreds of enslaved people reached Dunmore’s lines, and some served in what he called his “Ethiopian Regiment.” Many died of smallpox and other diseases. Patriot leaders and newspapers denounced the offer, and it hardened opposition to royal government among Virginia’s slaveholders.

Document Text

By His Excellency the Right Honorable JOHN Earl of DUNMORE, His Majesty’s Lieutenant and Governor General of the Colony and Dominion of Virginia, and Vice Admiral of the same.

A PROCLAMATION.

AS I have ever entertained Hopes, that an Accommodation might have taken Place between Great-Britain and this Colony, without being compelled by my Duty to this most disagreeable but now absolutely necessary Step, rendered so by a Body of armed Men unlawfully assembled, firing on His Majesty’s Tenders, and the formation of an Army, and that Army now on their March to attack His Majesty’s Troops and destroy the well disposed Subjects of this Colony. To defeat such reasonable Purposes, and that all such Traitors, and their Abettors, may be brought to Justice, and that the Peace, and good Order of this Colony may be again restored, which the ordinary Course of the Civil Law is unable to effect; I have thought fit to issue this my Proclamation, hereby declaring, that until the aforesaid good Purposes can be obtained, I do in Virtue of the Power and Authority to ME given, by His Majesty, determine to execute Martial Law, and cause the same to be executed throughout this Colony: and to the [word omitted in transcription] that Peace and good Order may the sooner be restored, I do require every Person capable of bearing Arms, to resort to His Majesty’s STANDARD, or be looked upon as Traitors to His Majesty’s Crown and Government, and thereby become liable to the Penalty the Law inflicts upon such Offences; such as forfeiture of Life, confiscation of Lands, &c. &c. And I do hereby further declare all indented Servants, Negroes, or others, (appertaining to Rebels,) free that are able and willing to bear Arms, they joining His Majesty’s Troops as soon as may be, for the more speedily reducing this Colony to a proper Sense of their Duty, to His Majesty’s Crown and Dignity. I do further order, and require, all His Majesty’s Leige Subjects, to retain their Quitrents, or any other Taxes due or that may become due, in their own Custody, till such Time as Peace may be again restored to this at present most unhappy Country, or demanded of them for their former salutary Purposes, by Officers properly authorised to receive the same.

GIVEN under my Hand on board the Ship WILLIAM, off Norfolk, the 7th Day of November, in the SIXTEENTH Year of His Majesty’s Reign.

DUNMORE.

(GOD save the KING.)
