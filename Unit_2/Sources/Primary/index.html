Reading These Documents

Spelling, capitalization, and punctuation follow the edition reproduced. The long “s” of eighteenth-century printing has been modernized. The bracketed marker [. . .] shows where the reader omits text; ellipses without brackets belong to the source or its editor. Some documents survive only in later printings, reconstructions, or memoirs written decades after the events; the editorial notes flag those cases.

Citing These Documents

Use the full footnote citation the first time you cite a document and the shortened form thereafter. Replace the sample locator in each footnote model (article, section, letter number, entry date, or original page) with the locator that supports your claim. Reader page numbers are navigation aids, not original source pages; never cite them.

Where a document also appears in the Norton History Source Collection, the editorial note says so. Cite the edition you actually quote. Include only the documents you actually use in your own bibliography.
