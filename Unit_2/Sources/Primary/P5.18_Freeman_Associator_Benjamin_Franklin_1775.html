“A Freeman and Associator” to Benjamin Franklin
“A Freeman and Associator” (an anonymous member of Philadelphia’s volunteer militia) · [Between August 3 and 19?], 1775
 
TOC FIT Chapter 5 · Essay prompt 5.1 (What Really Explains the American Revolution?)
WHY HERE In this short letter an ordinary member of Philadelphia’s volunteer militia asks that military discipline be organized on “the principles of freedom” and speaks for what “the privates” would approve. Students can consider what it suggests about how the language of resistance was being applied to relations between officers and men inside the colonies.
Historical Context

Pennsylvania had no compulsory militia law in 1775, because the Quaker-influenced Assembly had long resisted one. After fighting began at Lexington and Concord, thousands of Philadelphians formed voluntary military companies called Associators. On June 30, 1775, the Assembly created a Committee of Safety, with Benjamin Franklin as its president, to organize the colony’s defense. That summer a subcommittee began drafting rules to govern the Associators, and the committee adopted articles on August 19.

This unsigned letter enclosed a plan for governing the Associators that has not survived. The editors of the Franklin Papers date it between August 3 and 19, 1775, and suggest that the writer feared the committee would copy the Continental Army’s articles of war. Those articles, adopted by Congress in June, gave officers sweeping power over enlisted men.

Editorial note: Text of The Papers of Benjamin Franklin as published on Founders Online; spelling and capitalization follow the manuscript. The date is the editors’ conjecture and is given in the date line above. The bracketed “[President?]” in the address line is the editors’ suggested reading. Document Text

Sir

As the laws of war are very arbitrary; and vest an absolute Power in the Hands of one Class of men, over the Lives of another who have no voice among them, and as the Association of freemen can and Ought to be conducted on the principles of freedom, the enclos’d plan is submitted to your Consideration and that of the Committee of Safety. The plan is partly that design’d for one of the Companies of this city before it was known that the Committee of Safety would undertake to form any plan, and it is now laid before you with the utmost humility and Deference, and without the most distant view or Desire of dictating or directing, but purely to lay before you a Plan which would be generally approv’d by the privates, and which is principally design’d to hint to you that the plan which would Approach nearest to that of a free cival Constitution would meet with the highest Approbation and be most likely to answer the ends of our Association.

The Plan is neither perfect nor intended as such. It is laid before you more to shew you that Plans have been thought of and something of this kind Generally Approv’d as far as it has been talk’d of, than with any other view. Submitting to your Superior Wisdom and discernment, I beg leave to subscribe myself Your Most Obedient Humble servant

A Freeman and Associator

To Mr. Benjn. Franklin Esqr. Present [President?]
