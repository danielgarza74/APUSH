Rule to Show Cause in Marbury v. Madison
Supreme Court of the United States (clerk’s record) · December 1801
 
TOC FIT Chapter 7 · Essay prompt 7.5 (Judicial Guardian or Judicial Overreach? Marbury v. Madison)
WHY HERE The Court’s first step in the case, ordering Secretary of State James Madison to explain why he should not deliver Marbury’s commission, shows how the dispute reached the Court.
Historical Context

In December 1801 William Marbury and three other men whom President Adams had named justices of the peace for the District of Columbia asked the Supreme Court for a writ of mandamus ordering Secretary of State James Madison to deliver their undelivered commissions. The Court responded with a rule directing Madison to show cause why the writ should not issue at its next term.

The legible opening of the record names “William Marbury vs. James Madison, Secretary of State of the United States” and recites the motion, the notice given to Madison, and Marbury’s affidavit that President John Adams had nominated him and the Senate had consented. Because Congress’s Judiciary Act of 1802 abolished the Court’s next session, the case was not argued until February 1803.
