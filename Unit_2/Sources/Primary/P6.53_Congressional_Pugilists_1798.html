TOC FIT Chapter 6 · Essay prompt 6.5 (Faction as Disease or Democracy? The Rise of Political Parties)
WHY HERE The cartoon records the Lyon–Griswold brawl on the House floor and lets students weigh how partisanship looked to contemporaries in 1798.
Historical Context

On January 30, 1798, Republican congressman Matthew Lyon of Vermont spat in the face of Federalist Roger Griswold of Connecticut after an exchange of insults. When the House failed to expel Lyon, Griswold attacked him with a cane on February 15; Lyon defended himself with fire tongs from the chamber’s fireplace.

This anonymous etching shows the fight in Congress Hall, Philadelphia, while members look on, and its captions put words in the onlookers’ mouths. Months later Lyon became one of the first people convicted under the Sedition Act.

Editorial note: Captions engraved on the print are small in reproduction; students should examine the Library of Congress’s high-resolution file for the dialogue.
