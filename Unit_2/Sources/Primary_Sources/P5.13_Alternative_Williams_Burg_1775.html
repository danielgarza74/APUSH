Primary Source P5.13
The Alternative of Williams-Burg
Attributed to Philip Dawe; published by Robert Sayer and John Bennett, London · February 16, 1775
 
TOC FIT Chapter 5 · Essay prompt 5.5 (Loyalism Reconsidered)
WHY HERE The print shows how Loyalists and British observers portrayed the coercion of dissenters by patriot committees, evidence for weighing Loyalism against the pressures placed on those who refused to join the resistance.
Historical Context

This London mezzotint, part of the same series as the Edenton print, satirizes enforcement of the Virginia Association, the colony’s boycott of British goods. Under the shadow of a gallows hung with a sack of feathers and a bucket of tar, well-dressed Virginians are pressed by a crowd to sign the association at a table set on a tobacco hogshead.

The title plays on the choice offered to reluctant merchants: sign, or face tar and feathers. The print reflects a British and Loyalist view of the resistance as mob coercion; it is a satire, not a record of a particular event in Williamsburg.

Editorial note: Attribution to Philip Dawe follows the Metropolitan Museum of Art. The Met holds the impression reproduced here; the Library of Congress holds another (LCCN 97514624).
