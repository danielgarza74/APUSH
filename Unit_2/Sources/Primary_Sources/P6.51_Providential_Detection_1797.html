Primary Source P6.51
The Providential Detection
Unknown artist · ca. 1797–1800
 
TOC FIT Chapter 6 · Essay prompt 6.5 (Faction as Disease or Democracy? The Rise of Political Parties); Essay prompt 6.3 (Hamilton vs. Jefferson)
WHY HERE This Federalist attack on Jefferson pairs with his 1796 letter to Philip Mazzei and shows how partisans turned a private letter into evidence of disloyalty.
Historical Context

Jefferson’s April 1796 letter to his friend Philip Mazzei, which criticized the Federalists and was read as an attack on Washington, was published in Europe and then reprinted in American newspapers in May 1797. Federalists used it for years to portray Jefferson as a Francophile and an enemy of the Constitution.

In this engraving Jefferson kneels at an “Altar to Gallic Despotism,” where a fire is fed by Paine’s Age of Reason, the works of French philosophers, and Republican newspapers. A paper marked “To Mazzei” drops from one hand while an eagle snatches the Constitution from the other, and the eye of Providence looks down on the scene. The artist is unknown; the owning library dates the print to about 1797–1800.
