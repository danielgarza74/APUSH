George Washington to the Philadelphia Ladies Committee
George Washington · February 13, 1781
 
TOC FIT Chapter 5 · Essay prompt 5.4 (Republican Motherhood or Persistent Patriarchy?)
WHY HERE The commander in chief’s thanks to the women who had raised money and sewn shirts for the Continental Army. Students can examine the language Washington uses for the women’s work, including “female patriotism” and “those softer domestic virtues,” and consider what it suggests about how women’s public activity was understood and where its limits were placed.
ESSAY PROMPTS 5.4
Historical Context

In June 1780 Esther de Berdt Reed, wife of Pennsylvania’s chief executive Joseph Reed, published a broadside, The Sentiments of an American Woman, and organized the Ladies Association of Philadelphia. Its members went door to door collecting contributions for the soldiers of the Continental Army. Reed proposed giving the soldiers money directly. Washington asked instead that the funds buy linen for shirts, and the women made the shirts themselves.

Reed died in September 1780. A committee of the association, which included Sarah Franklin Bache, finished the work and delivered more than two thousand shirts to the army that winter. This letter, written from Washington’s headquarters at New Windsor, New York, answers the committee’s letter accompanying the shirts. The “deceased friend” of the opening sentence is Reed.

Document Text

⟨New Windsor Feb. 13th 1781⟩

Ladies,

The benevolent office, which added lustre to the qualities that ornamented your deceased friend could not have descended to more zealous or more deserving successors.

The contributions of the association you represent have exceeded what could have been expected, and the spirit that animated the members of it entitles them to an equal place with any who have preceded them in the walk of female patriotism. It embellishes the American character with a new trait; by proving that the love of Country is blended with those softer domestic virtues, which have always been allowed to be more peculiarly your own.

⟨You have not acquired admiration in your own country⟩ only; it is paid you abroad; & you will learn with pleasure by a part of your own Sex, where female accomplishments have attained their highest perfection—and who from the commencement have been the patronesses of American liberty.

The Army ought not to regret its sacrafices or its sufferings when they meet with so flattering a reward as in the sympathy of your Sex; nor can it fear that its interests will be neglected, while espoused by advocates as powerful as they are amiable.

I can only answer to the sentiments which you do me the honor to express for me personally, that they would more than repay a life devoted to the Service of the public & to testimonies of gratitude to yourselves. Accept the assurances of the perfect respect and esteem with which I am Ladies Yr Most Obedt—obliged—& Hble Servt Go: Washington
