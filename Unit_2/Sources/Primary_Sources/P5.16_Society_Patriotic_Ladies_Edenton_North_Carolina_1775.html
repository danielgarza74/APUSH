Primary Source P5.16
A Society of Patriotic Ladies, at Edenton in North Carolina
Attributed to Philip Dawe; published by Robert Sayer and John Bennett, London · March 25, 1775
 
TOC FIT Chapter 5 · Essay prompt 5.1 (What Really Explains the American Revolution?)
WHY HERE A London satire of colonial women’s boycott politics lets students examine how the non-importation movement drew in women and how British observers ridiculed that participation.
ESSAY PROMPTS 5.1
Historical Context

In October 1774 about fifty women in Edenton, North Carolina, signed a resolution supporting the Continental Congress’s boycott of British goods, including tea. News of the “Edenton Tea Party” reached London, where the print sellers Robert Sayer and John Bennett published this mezzotint in March 1775 as part of a series of satirical prints on the American crisis.

The print mocks the signers: some women appear coarse or flirtatious, one is courted by a man, a child sits neglected under the table, and a dog urinates nearby. The satire treats women’s political action as unnatural, but the fact that London printmakers bothered to ridicule it also shows that the boycott movement had reached into households.

Editorial note: The women’s actual resolution is not reproduced here; the paper being signed in the print is the artist’s invention. Attribution to Philip Dawe follows the Library of Congress catalog.
