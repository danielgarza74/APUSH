Ograbme, or, The American Snapping-Turtle
Attributed to Alexander Anderson · 1807–1808
 
TOC FIT Chapter 7 · Essay prompt 7.4 (Necessary War or War of Choice? The War of 1812)
WHY HERE An opponent’s cartoon of the embargo shows how some Americans judged Jefferson’s alternative to war: as a policy that bit its own citizens.
ESSAY PROMPTS 7.4
Historical Context

“Ograbme” is “embargo” spelled backward. In this woodcut a snapping turtle labeled with the name clamps onto a smuggler trying to carry a barrel of goods to a waiting British ship. The smuggler curses the “cursed Ograbme,” while another figure exclaims at how it nips.

The image is usually attributed to the New York engraver Alexander Anderson. It circulated among Federalist critics of the Embargo Act of 1807, which halted American foreign trade and fell hardest on seaport merchants and sailors.
