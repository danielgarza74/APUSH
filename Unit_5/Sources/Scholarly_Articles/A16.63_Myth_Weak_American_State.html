Selection Overview

Novak argues that Americans narrate their history as a story of limited government and freedom even as state power grows. He cites Albert Shaw, who in 1887 mocked a laissez-faire fantasy after surveying thousands of state regulatory laws, and Senator Hollings, whose 1984 stump speech described a man blind to how government programs built his life.

He lives in one wo theory and in another world of practice... Never for a moment relinquishing th theory [of laissez-faire], the people of the United States have assiduously pu and cherished a practical policy utterly inconsistent with that theory, and have perceived the discrepancy." Surveying thousands of regulatory laws passed by s legislatures in the late nineteenth century, Shaw concluded that "the one co and striking characteristic of this huge collection of new statutes is its utter dis of the laissez-faire principle ...
