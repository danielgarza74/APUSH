Andrew C. Isenberg

JOURNAL Great Plains Quarterly 12, no. 4 (1992)
EXCERPT Original journal pages 227-228
JSTOR https://www.jstor.org/stable/23531659
Selection Overview

Isenberg argues that the near-extermination of the bison between the Louisiana Purchase in 1803 and 1883 was purposeful, not a simple failure of resource management, because the federal government sought to pacify Plains Indians by destroying the herds. He links this to a Darwinian worldview casting nature as competitive and Indians as doomed, which displaced Jeffersonian ideas of an orderly nature.
