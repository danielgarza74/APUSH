A close reading of their works suggests that these historians have put forward three distinct, though not necessarily incompatible, theories to explain how cultural impulses become politi- cal ones: (i) negative reference group antagonisms, (2) conflicts of cus- tom and life-style, and (3) differences in religious values and world views. Moreover, ethno-culturalists do not agree completely on such po- litical concerns as the linkage between voter mobilization and policy making. While no general appraisal of ethno-cultural history is yet pos- sible, it is time for at least an interim evaluation of the contribution made by these historians to our understanding of past political behavior.2 Political History as Social History Ethno-cultural history represents, in part, a response to earlier schools of historiography.
