PRIMARY SOURCE S12.48
The Tree of Temperance and The Tree of Intemperance
N. Currier (lithographers and publishers) · 1849 (New York)
 
Field Detail
TOC Fit Chapter 12 · Essay prompt 12.2 (Why Reform?)
Why Here A matched pair of cheap, hand-colored prints that show the “fruits” of sobriety and of drink as moral, religious, and economic outcomes at once: thrift, home, and church on one side; poverty, crime, and ruin on the other. Students can read them as an argument about how reformers linked self-control to prosperity and respectability, and ask who bought such pictures and why.
Treatment Public-domain images (Library of Congress); printed titles, verses, and trunk and root labels transcribed from the images
Historical Context
The temperance crusade was the largest reform movement of the antebellum era. Evangelical ministers and the American Temperance Society (founded 1826) first urged moderation and then total abstinence, and per capita drinking of spirits fell sharply between 1830 and 1850. In the 1840s the Washingtonian movement drew in reformed drinkers, many of them artisans and laborers, and women’s auxiliaries spread the pledge through homes and churches. Reformers argued that drink caused poverty, crime, and the abuse of wives and children, and that sober workers would rise.
Nathaniel Currier’s New York firm (later Currier & Ives) sold inexpensive hand-colored lithographs on every popular subject, from disasters and politics to sentimental scenes. Temperance pictures sold well, and Currier issued paired “before and after” or “tree” prints in which branches bore labeled fruits. Two years after this pair appeared, Maine passed the first statewide prohibition law (1851).
Editorial note. The two prints were issued as a pair by N. Currier in 1849 (Gale Currier & Ives catalogue raisonné nos. 6622 and 6614). The American Yawp Reader shows the American Antiquarian Society’s copies; this reader uses the Library of Congress copies of the same prints, which carry a “no known restrictions on publication” rights statement. The labels on the fruits are printed very small and are not separately transcribed here; read them from the images. The titles, Bible verses, and the larger trunk and root labels are transcribed below. The images reproduced are the Library of Congress’s color master scans of these prints (catalog records 2002697364 and 2002697360). Document Text
Transcription of the printed text: The Tree of Temperance
THE TREE OF TEMPERANCE.
Ye shall know them by their fruits. Do men gather grapes of thorns, or figs of thistles? Matt. VII. 16. Ho, every one that thirsteth, come ye to the waters, Isaiah LV. 1. And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess, Ephesians V. 18.
And if he be thirsty give him water to drink, Prov. [illegible]. And he shall be like a tree planted by the rivers of water, that bringeth forth fruit in his season; his leaf also shall not wither; and whatsoever he doeth shall prosper. Psalms I. 3.
On the trunk and limbs: Health. Prosperity. Happiness. Strength of Mind. Strength of B[illegible].
Lith. & Pub. by N. Currier. 152 Nassau St. Cor. of Spruce N.Y.
Transcription of the printed text: The Tree of Intemperance
THE TREE OF INTEMPERANCE.
Wine is a mocker, Strong drink is raging, and whosoever is deceived thereby is not wise. Proverbs XX. 1. At the last it biteth like a Serpent, and stingeth like an Adder. Prov. XXIII. 32. For the Glutton and the Drunkard shall come to Poverty and drowsiness shall clothe a man with rags. Proverbs XXIII. 21.
On the trunk and limbs: Poverty. Insanity. Misery. Disease. Coiled around the trunk: Alcohol. On the roots: Brandy. Gin. Rum. Wine. Beer. Cider. Sign over the shop door: Liquors & W[illegible].
Lith. & Pub. by N. Currier. 152 Nassau St. Cor. of Spruce N.Y.
