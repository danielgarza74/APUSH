TOC Fit Chapter 8 · Essay prompt 8.5 (The Roots of Nativism)
Why Here An illustrated advertisement for a Boston nativist newspaper lists what the paper stands for, putting “protection of American Mechanics Against Foreign Pauper Labor” and a twenty-one-year wait for naturalization beside attacks on “Papal Agression,” nunneries, and Jesuits. Students can weigh economic and religious grievances that the same publisher packaged together for working-class readers.
Treatment Public-domain image; printed passages as transcribed in the repository record
Historical Context
Boston received tens of thousands of Irish famine immigrants between 1846 and 1854, many of whom found work only as day laborers and domestic servants, and the city’s cost of poor relief rose sharply. Native-born artisans and laborers complained of wage competition, while Protestant ministers and editors warned that Catholic immigrants would answer to the pope rather than to the republic. Small nativist newspapers and lodges spread through the city in the early 1850s.
This broadside, published by J. E. Farwell & Co. of Congress Street in 1852, advertised a new nativist paper. Its illustration contrasts orderly native-born Americans gathered beneath a temple of Liberty with disorderly Irish immigrants landing from a ship “from Cork” under the papal arms. Two years later Massachusetts voters elected a Know-Nothing governor and nearly every member of the legislature.
Editorial note. The plan identified this item from an education website (Re-Imagining Migration) as an advertisement for the “American Citizen”; the Library of Congress record, opened directly, shows that the paper advertised is named in the print’s title as “the American Patriot,” though the catalog summary calls it the “American Citizen.” CAUTION: This is advertising for a partisan newspaper, built to provoke; its picture of immigrants is a caricature, and it states the paper’s program, not evidence of actual wages or immigrant behavior. Text transcribed for this reader from the repository image; illegible words are marked [illegible]. The print reads “Aggression,” not the catalog’s “Agression.”
Document Text
Transcription of the printed text
AMERICAN CITIZENS! We appeal to you in all calmness. Is it not time to pause? Already the enemies of our dearest institutions, like the foreign spies in the Trojan horse of old, are within our gates. They are disgorging themselves upon us, at the rate of HUNDREDS OF THOUSANDS EVERY YEAR! They aim at nothing short of conquest and supremacy over us.
A PAPER ENTITLED THE AMERICAN PATRIOT.
[Lettering in the picture, left side:] E Pluribus Unum. Liberty. Constitution and Laws. The Bible [illegible] of Liberty. [illegible] Foreign Influence. [Flag: illegible.] Lexington Concord Bunker-Hill. [Right side:] We are bound to carry out the pious intentions of His Holiness the Pope. [illegible] to advance the Faith. Americans shant rule us!! Fradom of spache an' action!! Roma.
IN FAVOR OF The protection of American Mechanics against Foreign Pauper Labor. Foreigners having a residence in the country of 21 years before voting. Our present Free School System. Carrying out the laws of the State, as regards sending back Foreign Paupers and Criminals.
OPPOSED TO Papal Aggression & Roman Catholicism. Foreigners holding office. Raising Foreign Military Companies in the United States. Nunneries and the Jesuits. To being taxed for the support of Foreign paupers millions of dollars yearly. To secret Foreign Orders in the U. S.
We are burdened with enormous taxes by foreigners. We are corrupted in the morals of our youth. We are interfered with in our government. We are forced into collisions with other nations. We are tampered with in our religion. We are injured in our labor. We are assailed in our freedom of speech.
☞ The PATRIOT is Published by J. E. Farwell & Co., 32 Congress St., Boston, And for Sale at the Periodical Depots in this place. Single copies 4 Cents.
