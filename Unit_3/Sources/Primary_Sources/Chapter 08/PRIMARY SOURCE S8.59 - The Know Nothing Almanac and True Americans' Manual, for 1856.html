Historical Context
Almanacs were among the most widely owned printed works in nineteenth-century America, combining calendars and astronomical tables with miscellaneous reading. The New York publishers DeWitt & Davenport issued The Know Nothing Almanac and True Americans’ Manual for 1855 and again for 1856, edited by W. S. Tisdale. Besides the calendar, the 1856 issue carried anti-Catholic stories and pictures, articles on church property and convents, sketches of nativist leaders, and tables of election returns.
The almanac appeared at the end of 1855, after a year in which the American Party had won governorships and legislatures in several northern and border states but lost key races in Virginia and elsewhere. The editors addressed both committed party members and voters still loyal to the Democrats and Whigs, mixing Protestant religious warnings with arguments about wages and party corruption.

Preface.
The unprecedented success of our last year’s issue, induces us to continue the publication of our True Americans’ Almanac and Hand-Book of reference for political Native Americans, and we hope our patriotic friends will continue to patronize their own Almanac, in order that its field of usefulness may be extended.
There is no method for the extension of knowledge and the promulgation of principles more powerful and efficient than that of the tract system; and as this little work is sold at a very low price (especially in large quantities), it may be assumed that our little Almanac is an American and Protestant tract. Let those true patriots, then, who sincerely desire to extend a knowledge of our principles, invest either one shilling or one dollar in so good a cause, and those who can afford it could not expend even a larger amount in a more effectual manner for the dissemination of our political doctrines than in the purchase of this truly proselyting pamphlet. By distributing them broadcast over the land, an incalculable amount of good may be accomplished; and especially if our true American friends will send copies to those of their deluded countrymen, who are still in the chains of party, and continue to believe in the honesty and truthfulness of those demagogues, whose trade, profession, and practice of politics compel them to deceive, to cheat, and betray the credulous people.
It is only by such documentary evidence as we have compiled for the Almanac, that the people can be properly informed as to our American purposes, and in no other way can we reach the individual attention and final judgment of the people, touching the patriotism of our political tenets, so directly as by this method of placing the facts before their eyes. In view of the correctness of our arguments in favor of a wide circulation of our Almanac and Manual, we contend that it is a duty incumbent upon every true American, and every sincere Protestant, to take a personal interest in the work, and see that his immediate neighborhood is well supplied with copies. Our enemies are constantly at work, endeavoring to divide and distract us—our inveterate foes, the leaders of all that remains of the old parties, never forego an opportunity to injure us—and it is only by offsetting their infamous misrepresentations, with documentary evidences in the form of statistics, &c., that we can make head against the Romish falsifiers of the truth, and their allies, the native traitors.
You, readers, should do your duty by sending a copy of this Manual to any friend who is still a member of the tory parties; for now there is but one American party, and its opponents are in the same position of antagonism to the patriot party as the tories were to the patriot army, during the revolutionary struggle of our forefathers. Let our opponents torture and distort the truth as they may, no specious reasoning, no political sophistry can alter the fact that those who are constantly laboring to fight down Americanism and Protestantism are enemies to their country, and tories or traitors to their native land.
A correct understanding of our principles is all that is required to set the people aright on this subject; and if every American would do his share towards accomplishing the peaceful revolution we are laboring to consummate, then the work will be completed without the necessity of resorting to a more desperate alternative. That “Revolutions never go backward,” is an axiom as unerring as fate, and the revolution having for its aim the deliverance of our country from all foreign influence, at home and abroad, will sweep on to its final victory, even though its course be through the quivering hearts of our alien foes. Avert this dire necessity, this desperate and inevitable step, ye who prate of peace while there is no peace—ye who censure Americans for avenging the death of their brother Americans—ye who preach submission, to the hordes of foreign ingrates, swarming like vermin through the land—ye who have helped to “make your father’s house a den of thieves”—ye who, “worse than the Infidel, provide not for your own household,” but squander your rich inheritance upon the unworthy stranger—avert the final appeal to arms, and seal not your infamy in the blood of your native land.

To the American Mechanics, Working Men, Women, and Sewing-Girls, Living in Cities and Towns.
Why are you poor? This is a question which must interest you, and if you will read these few pages carefully, intelligently, and free from the curse of political bias and prejudice, you will find the answer; and if you are wise, you will act upon the suggestions conveyed in that answer.
When the market of labor is not glutted, then the pay for labor is high, because a scarcity of hands causes high wages; but if the labor-mart is flooded to repletion with hands ready to work for fifty cents a day, then fifty cents a day will be the prevailing price. This is common sense, but it is not political humbug and falsehood. That the competition of foreign cheap labor in the American labor-market is ruinous, is a fact which none but political rascals deny, none but scoundrelly demagogues gainsay.
Well then, what follows?
If competition is ruinous, it is desirable to know where that competition comes from, of what it consists, and how it is to be got rid of.
Ruinous competition comes from Europe; it consists of men and women who are ready to work for fifty cents a day, and the way to get rid of it is to vote for some measure which will effectually check the flood of cheap laborers pouring into the American labor-market from abroad.
Reader, if you are a Democrat, you know very well that there is no measure advocated by the Democratic leaders that has this object in view; and if you are a Whig, you know that the leading men of your party are in favor of leaving the flood-gates of emigration open, so that cheap laborers may pour in and drive you from your employments by their ruinous competition.
We will suppose, for the sake of argument, that you are in the receipt of $2 50 per day, or, to be more liberal still, say you are earning $800 per annum. Then, we will say, you are paying only $250 a year for rent; that you have two sons and a daughter whom you wish to educate, and that they are going to one of our noble free schools, where they can get a good education and have the influence of the Word of God besides. But the expense of books, clothing, eating, &c., &c., will amount to at least $200 for the three. You want furniture, cooking utensils, &c., which will cost say $200, a low estimate. Then your own and your wife’s expenses for clothing, &c., $200. Already, in these low estimates, your income is exhausted, while there are many incidentals which Americans need and are entitled to that we have not named. But we will suppose that you can live on your income of $800 per annum, and educate and clothe your children besides. Now we ask if a mere existence, a living from hand to mouth, year in and year out, is all that is required in this life? What provision are you making for old age? What are you laying up for a rainy day? How will you be able to meet your doctor’s bills in case of sickness?
Now, in a republican country, you can vote for or against the evils under which you suffer, and if you vote for their continuance, you have no one to blame but yourselves. American mechanics; does the competition of foreign sewing women injure your wives’ or your daughters’ calling? Vote for a restriction upon the emigration of foreign working women. Does the competition of foreign cheap laborers tend to reduce your wages? Vote for a check upon the tremendous influx of cheap laborers flooding the domestic labor-market. This is the remedy. Redress is to be had at the ballot-box, but as long as you are blind partizans of some merely political organization, you will not see where the evil lies, and where the remedy is to be had, and how it is to be applied.
This is an appeal to your parental feelings as well as to your patriotism.
Your vote, when thrown away upon mere party measures, which are not calculated to benefit either you, or your family, or your country, is worse than useless, whilst its judicious use would help to carry out a measure or a principle which would materially benefit you, your family, and your country.
It is a burning shame that Americans will allow themselves to be so grossly imposed upon by heartless politicians and unprincipled demagogues, as to go blindly to the ballot-box, year after year, and vote for the personal aggrandisement of a set of selfish men, who represent no principle but the principle of self. Many men rush to the ballot-box as though their very salvation depended upon voting; as though their lives were at stake, and yet they could not, if their existence depended upon it, name a single principle involved in the act.
We have known Americans to be driven from their employments by cheap-working foreigners. We have witnessed the sufferings of their families, in consequence of being deprived of work, and yet those very Americans would go and vote for a continuance of that very competition which had brought distress upon themselves and families! Is not this criminal as well as foolish? To vote for the importation of one hundred thousand cheap-working men and women yearly is suicidal in the extreme.
If men, who alone have the power to vote down a crying evil, were only injuring themselves by their blind zeal for party, there might be some shadow of an excuse for them; but when the welfare of their wives, daughters, and sons depends upon a rational understanding of what mischief or benefit their votes are likely to produce, there is no longer any excuse for a rash, thoughtless, and irrational use of the elective franchise.
We know an American mechanic who belongs to that school of politics called “Democratic” (but what that means now-a-days it is impossible to say) and he has adhered to it, not knowing why he should, through ruin and disaster caused by the competition and underworking of foreigners in his branch of business. Always a Democrat—not an American Democrat, opposed to ruinous foreign competition, but a Democrat in the most unmeaning political sense of the word. A “Democrat” or a “Whig,” according to the interpretation of political demagogues, means—the man who votes for a set of candidates put up by the Democratic or Whig nominating caucus; no principles; no reformatory measures; but men.
Be not deceived by such palpable frauds any longer, but think and act for yourselves, or, if you rely upon the opinions of some man, who is called a “leader,” or upon the opinions of some “great statesman,” as many corrupt and selfish demagogues are called, apply to him for an explanation of the principles and measures of your party, and get him to explain, if he can, how you and your country are benefited by your vote. Ask him if, by voting the Whig or Democratic ticket, you will get rid of the greatest curse that has befallen you—that of the competition of hosts of cheap-working foreigners who surround you and crowd you out of your employment. There is only one remedy for the evil of foreign competition in the home labor-market, and that remedy is your vote. There is only one party that advocates a restriction upon the flood of pauper and other cheap laborers from Europe, and, to be consistent as well as merciful to your own dependent families, you must of necessity vote with that party, but, at the same time, you are at perfect liberty to go on voting for a continuance of emigration until the pauper prices of Europe prevail in the American labor-market. It is a “free country,” and men who will persist in voting for low wages cannot be interfered with.
