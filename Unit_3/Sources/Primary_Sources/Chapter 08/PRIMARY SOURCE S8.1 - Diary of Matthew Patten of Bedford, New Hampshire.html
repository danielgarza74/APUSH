Why Here Two months in the life of a New Hampshire farmer, surveyor, and justice of the peace: he sells fish for cash, trades fish for salt and rum, settles accounts in old-tenor shillings, takes payment in corn and rye, and buys on credit. Students can test whether rural New England was already tied to money and markets long before 1815.

Historical Context
Matthew Patten (1719–1795) was born in Ireland and came to New England as a child with his Scots-Irish family. He settled in Bedford, New Hampshire, on the Merrimack River, where he farmed, made furniture and wooden dishes, surveyed land, wrote deeds and wills, and served as justice of the peace, legislator, and judge of probate. His terse daily diary survives from 1754 to 1788 and was printed by the town of Bedford in 1903.
Like most country people of the period, Patten dealt in several currencies at once: lawful money (“La Mo”), the inflated paper “old Tenor” of earlier years, Spanish dollars, and goods. Neighbors kept running accounts, settled them periodically, and paid in labor, produce, and credit at local stores. In spring he and his sons fished for shad and salmon at Amoskeag Falls, and the entries below show how the catch moved through sale, barter, and payment of debts. In his figures, 4:8:0 means four pounds, eight shillings, no pence.
Editorial note. This reader reproduces eleven entries from May 22 to July 25, 1767 (pp. 195–98 of the 1903 printing), transcribed from the Internet Archive scan’s OCR text and corrected by the compiler. Patten’s spelling and lack of punctuation are kept. The 1903 printing sets many abbreviations with raised letters (Recd, Jona, Thos, 10d); these are printed here on the line. The pound-weight sign is given as “lb.” Readings in square brackets are figures the OCR garbled and the compiler could not confirm against the page image; they should be checked against the scan before classroom use. Each entry is headed by its date, supplied from the diary’s day numbers and month headings. CAUTION: These entries come from 1767, half a century before the unit’s 1815–1850 focus; they are baseline evidence for comparison, not evidence of the market revolution itself.

May 22, 1767
Recd 3 Hundred of 10d Nails from Jona Currier that he brot from Mr Gordons for me on Esqr Rands acct
May 25, 1767
I finished makeing and pitching the Canno for myself and the one for Esq Rand and I borrowed about a gallon of Tar from Capt Goffes wife
May 26, 1767
I Coppyd of Caldwells and Walkers case for Walker and Walkers acct for Caldwell and John got 3 bushell of corn from Moses Vickery
May 28, 1767
I went to fish at Amoskeeg and I fished untill the 6th of June at night and in that time I sent home 354 and I got 60 of Jonas that he would not sell and 38 of John I gave 100 for [2½] bushell of salt and 60 I let Ensn Daniel Moor have toward what I owe him and 20 I let Capt Walker and my bror have 120 I sold for money and a hoe 10 I gave Robt McMurphy and 10 I let Gan Riddell have toward [½] a Gallon of Rum I had from him last summer
June 8–12, 1767
8th 9th 10th 11th and 12th in the forenoon I with Jas Patterson Ebenr Hacket and Alexr McMurphy we workt at our fishing place between the Pulpit and Sullivans Point we Settled our acct Relateing to the makeing it and I had 2:19:6 comeing to me Hacket was to pay me 2:4:6 and there was 15/ in my hand by 5 shad that I sold and he owes me 1:9:6 all old Tenor And I bot a Barrell of Cyder from Capt Walker and my bror for a Dollar and I pd them the money I owe the bringing it on to the Island from the East side yet
June 27, 1767
I carried a Note of 4:4:9 La Mo: to against Lieut Josiah Brown to Amoskieg and sent it from there by Ensn McAllan to Capt John Stark to Receive the money for me and there was 3/3 La: Mo: to be added makes 4:8:0 prinsaple and Intrest on it for 13 months And I bot a bottle of snuff from Thos Russ boy for 40/ old Tenor and I pd for it and in the morning James Patterson brot me 3 Salmon that weighed 20 pound & Thos McLaughlin had one that weighed 15 lb and Patterson had half a salmon
July 3, 1767
I went to Matthew Littles and Writ a Deed from Christopher Williams to Jotham Tuttle and another from sd Williams to Geo Hadley and another from sd Tuttle to sd Hadley and took the acknowledgements of the 3 and an Instrument from Tuttle to Hadley for Tuttle to lift a mortgage that lay on the land he Deeded to him and I Drawed an Acct for Tuttle against Josiah Folensbe and swore him to it Williams is to pay me one shilling Lawful mo and Hadley is to pay me 3/ La: Mo: in Corn and Tuttle paid me one shilling La: Mo: and he owes me 2/ yet and I bot a Scyth from Matthew Little on Cr
July 4, 1767
I went along with John Clogston and Benj Davis and Run Round the lott no 2 in the second Rang on the south side of Piscataquog River in Goffstown and Divided it in equal halfs between them Clogston is to give me a bushell of Rie for his part and Davis to let me have as much for his part for my service
July 17, 1767
we finished and both partys aquiesed and Exchanged Receipts and Capt Greele gave me a Dollar for my pay and I came home by dark
July 20, 1767
I paid Francis Barnet 4-10-0 old Tenor for a barrel of Cyder I had from him the 14th of last February and I had John Clogstons oxen ploughing and he had my horse to plough among his Corn in their Room
July 25, 1767
I finished ploughing the old field before the house by noon I had Kidders steers helping me to finish and yesterday Susannah got [8½] lb of pork from James Littles wife for [6] Dishes she got the 23d and she sent us a pig for a Roaster and I sent John to Ensign Moors mill and got 3 bushell of Rie ground
