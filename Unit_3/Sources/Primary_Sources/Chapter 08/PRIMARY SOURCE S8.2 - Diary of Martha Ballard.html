Diary of Martha Ballard (selected entries on buying, selling, and payment)
Martha Ballard · 1785–1812 (manuscript diary, Hallowell, Maine)
 
Field Detail
TOC Fit Chapter 8 · Essay prompt 8.1 (Revolution or Evolution?)
Why Here A rural midwife’s daily notes record fees paid in cash, cloth, flatirons, chocolate, and store credit, along with loans of meat and settled accounts. Students can use it to judge how deeply an ordinary farm household was tied to exchange, credit, and money well before the canal and railroad era.
Treatment Public-domain manuscript in a modern verbatim transcription, selected entries
Historical Context
Martha Moore Ballard (1735–1812) worked as a midwife and healer in Hallowell (later Augusta), Maine, on the Kennebec River, which was then part of Massachusetts. From 1785 until shortly before her death she kept a terse daily diary recording the weather, her household work, the births she attended (more than 800), and the comings and goings of family and neighbors. Her husband Ephraim was a surveyor and mill owner.
Like most rural New Englanders of the period, the Ballards lived in an economy short of coin. Debts were kept in shillings and pence long after the dollar became official, and neighbors paid one another with goods, labor, store orders, and promissory notes as well as cash. The diary entries below record fees, purchases, loans, and settlements of account. In Ballard’s notation, 6/ means six shillings, /9d nine pence, and 2:13:4 two pounds, thirteen shillings, four pence; “ye” and “yt” are “the” and “that,” and “the hook” is the nearby village of Hallowell Hook.
Editorial note. The diary manuscript is held by the Maine State Library. This reader reproduces sixteen dated entries from the verbatim transcription by Robert R. and Cynthia MacAlman McCausland published on the DoHistory website (Film Study Center, Harvard University), taken from the site’s “Martha Ballard and Money” theme pages, which collect entries on payment and exchange. The McCauslands kept Ballard’s spelling and abbreviations; their uncertain readings appear in square brackets. Only the main diary text is given: the day-and-weekday numbers and Ballard’s marginal notes (birth tallies and fee memoranda) are omitted, and superscript letters are printed on the line. Later notes she added to an entry, such as “receivd 7/4 march 18 1794,” are part of the transcribed entry. CAUTION: These entries fall before the 1815–1850 focus of the unit; they show the rural economy the market revolution grew out of, not the revolution itself.
Document Text
November 12, 1785, Saturday
I am at home. Doct Wms Calld here on his return from mr Babcocks. Enforms me yt She is Some more Comfortable; he has hopes She may recover. mr Smily here. we bt 18 lb of Buttr of him. we paid him one Dollar in Cash & three at Davess Store; we are to give one Shilling pr pound.
September 2, 1786, Saturday
I have been at home. Benjamin White Brot me a pair of Flat Irons, prs 6/, which is my fee, 2/ remains, Due for remidies. Dolly is 14 years of age this Day. Parthena Barton here. mr Ballard mett ye Select Gentlemen at mr Pollards this fore noon.
October 10, 1786, Tuesday
a clear day. I Came from mr Porters at Sun rise, was at mr Westons, Pollards, & Brookses. Bt a Black handkercf of a Dutchmn, a Case of knives & forks of mr Brooke. have had much Labour to perform ys Day. Sister Williams Came here about 1/O Clok. Jonn killd a Steer. Cyrus went to ye hook for some Earthen ware. mr Pollard gave a dollar which was Left for me by a man from winthp.
February 28, 1787, Wednesday
Cloudy, a little Snow. mr Ballard at mr Fosters. I Combd 10 lb of flax. Pamila finisht her skirt & Jackat. Joseph Foster had 1-1/2 oz tinctr, /9d. he carrid 20 pills to mr Childs.
July 21, 1787, Saturday
Clear. I went to See David Densmoores knee. mr Barton & Betsy Town went from here. I Settld with Pamela This Day. Shee has Lost 1 months time, or rather workt for her Self since Shee came here & had, 20 yd s Cloth at 2/8 pr yd 2:13:4 Lawn for an apron : 7:6 2 pair shoes 12/ Leather & makeing :17:1 Paid mrs Densmore for Cuting her gown :6 Credit by her Labour 4:--:- Detr to 1/7 in cash : 1:7
January 29, 1790, Friday
Clear & very Cold. I was Calld to Moses Sewalls wife in travil at 1 h morn. Shee was Delivrd at 4 of a Son. I Left her Cleverly after Dinner. receivd 1 lb Chocolat & 1/2 lb Tea. he paid 2/8 at Arnals Store for me.
January 14, 1793, Monday
Clear. mr Ballard & Cyrus went to Town meeting. I Bakt & Brewd & was Calld to See mrs Thwing at 6h Evn. Shee was Deld at 10 off her first Born, a Son. I was waited for by mr Brown to attend mrs Suell who we arivd there at 11 30m. Shee was Deld off a Son at 12. I tarried there thro the night. my Patient is Cleverly. receivd 7/4 march 18 1794.
June 27, 1793, Thursday
Clear & warm. I was Calld to Thomas Stickneys wife in Labour at 8h morn. Shee was Safe Delivd at 10 of a Son, her first Born. I receivd 6/ as reward, left her Cleverly at 3 pm & returnd. Cyrus Sleeps here. Borrowed 13-3/4 lb veal of mr Densmr.
June 28, 1793, Friday
Clear Except a Sprinkling of rain. mr Ballard returnd, Cyrus Sleeps here. Sally went to Son Pollards, Ephm workt for mr Child. I have been at home, lent mr Densmr 6 lb, lacking 2 oz, of Pork.
June 17, 1795, Wednesday
Clear and warm. I went to the hook, paid mr Joseph Dummer 15 Dolrs for a Buro, Table and Bd Stead for Dolly. I Bot of mr Strattne 2 poringers @ 1/9, one 1/6 - 5/ -, 2 Candlesticks 1/4; at Rold Smiths 2 Kenisters 2/8, the whole 9/. I went to See mrs Waid whose Oldest Child or Dagt Expird at 10h this morn. mr Ballard has been Surveying for mr Child and Patridg. Ephm Brot Dollys furniture from mr Dumers.
March 26, 1796, Saturday
Clear. Cyrus went to the hook, was informd that mr Daniel Stevens was Dead and that his remains were to be interd this aftern. my Son Ephm here, he went to pittstn for money Due to Cyrus from Rheuben Moore. he brot it to him and he Sent it to his Bror Lambart, 10 Dollars and 2 pisterenes. I have been at home.
April 24, 1800, Thursday
Snowd this forenn. Lydia Nud helpt me wash. Hepsy workt Some for her Self. mr Kittridge here and Setled with mr Ballard, gave him a due bill for ye ballance which was Two Dols 17 Cents.
June 3, 1800, Tuesday
Clear. Sabery Price Came and helpt me thro with my washing. I workt Some in my Gardin, was Calld at Evng to See mrs Gill, find his Marm and mrs Pitts there. I laid down and Slept a little. Dagt Ballard & mrs Chamberlin here to Tea. I let ye latter have a pair of Leather Shoes for which Shee is to Spinn me 15 Double Skeins yarn.
February 13, 1807, Friday
Clear part of the day. Deac Page, mr Woodward, mr Bullen & wife here. mr Ballard gone to mill. mr Wdwd brot me Some mitk & Chees. Deacn Page gave me 20 Cents.
June 24, 1807, Wednesday
Clear Part of ye day. I have workt in my Gardin, over hauled wollin raggs, Cut Some for flock & Some for Carpet filling. mr Ballard been to the Settlement. Rachel Smith Paid [in] 1 Dollar in part of what was Due to me for asisting her in Child [Bed].
April 4, 1812, Saturday
Clear. I was Calld at 1h this morn by mr Cleark to See his wife in Labour. I wrode on hors back with out a pillion, felt very much fatagued. the Patient was Safe Deld at 3h pm of her fifth Son. I tarried all night.
