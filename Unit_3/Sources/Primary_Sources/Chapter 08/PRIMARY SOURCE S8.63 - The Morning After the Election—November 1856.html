The Morning After the Election—November 1856

Field Detail
TOC Fit Chapter 8 · Essay prompt 8.5 (The Roots of Nativism)
Why Here A Democratic-leaning cartoon made just after the 1856 election shows the winner, James Buchanan, celebrating that no one will “breathe Disunion,” while Millard Fillmore, the American Party candidate, laments being thrown “into the dark & gloomy caverns of Know Nothingism.” Students can weigh how contemporaries placed the Know-Nothings within the collapse of the old two-party system.
Treatment Public-domain image; speech balloons as transcribed in the repository record
Historical Context
The presidential election of 1856 was a three-way race. The Democrats nominated James Buchanan of Pennsylvania; the new Republican Party, opposed to the spread of slavery into the territories, ran the explorer John C. Frémont; and the American (Know-Nothing) Party, with support from the remnant of the Whigs, ran former president Millard Fillmore on a platform of saving the Union. Buchanan won with 45 percent of the popular vote, Frémont carried eleven northern states, and Fillmore won about 21 percent of the vote but only Maryland’s electoral votes.
The result showed that the Republicans, not the Know-Nothings, had replaced the Whigs as the main opposition in the North, and the American Party soon broke apart. In the cartoon, Buchanan sits among farm tools beside ripe wheat, the editors who backed Frémont chase after him with unpaid bills, and Fillmore, holding a lantern (a Know-Nothing symbol), blames Ned Buntline (Edward Z. C. Judson), a writer and nativist organizer.

Document Text
Speech balloons (as transcribed in the Library of Congress catalog record)
Buchanan: "What a happy morning for my country and myself. Here I find returns for myself & my Kentucky brother--beginning with Maine in the North & concluding with Texas in the South. What welcome news to know that the People have not removed a plank of the Democratic Platform. Who will dare breathe Disunion now?"
Frémont: "I'm off to Mariposa--Like a foolish fellow, you Editors made me believe papers could do all things--The people you see have used us up. When I get to my gold regions & "back again," I'll pay you "in a horn.""
Fillmore: "Oh! Ned! Ned! This is all of your doing. After being a popular Whig President--and walking in the footsteps of Clay, Webster & Cass. I am thrown back by the People into the dark & gloomy caverns of Know Nothingism."
