PRIMARY SOURCE S8.57
The Propagation Society. More Free than Welcome
Nathaniel Currier, publisher (probably drawn by Louis Maurer) · 1855 (lithograph)
 
Field Detail
TOC Fit Chapter 8 · Essay prompt 8.5 (The Roots of Nativism)
Why Here A popular New York print shows the pope and his bishops landing on American shores to seize the nation’s souls and money, and an American boy answering that he will “Know nothing” but the Bible. Students can read it as an argument by its maker about what Know-Nothings feared most, and ask how a pun on the party’s name tied religious fear to politics.
Treatment Public-domain image; speech balloons as transcribed in the repository record
Historical Context
Nathaniel Currier’s New York firm (later Currier & Ives) sold inexpensive hand-colored lithographs to a mass market, including many political cartoons. This print was entered for copyright in July 1855, when the Know-Nothing or American Party was at its height, having won control of the Massachusetts government and many city offices in 1854–55. Its title plays on the Society for the Propagation of the Faith, a Catholic missionary organization that sent funds to the church in the United States.
The cartoon draws on fears that had circulated since the 1830s: that the papacy sought political power in America, that Irish immigrants (the shamrock the bishops’ boat is hooked to) served as its instruments, and that Catholic leaders wanted to remove the Protestant Bible from public schools. Pope Pius IX had become a symbol of reaction to many Americans after he turned against the European revolutions of 1848.
Editorial note. The Norton Online Reader’s “Know-Nothing Cartoon” could not be consulted for this reader, so the specific image Norton uses could not be identified. This entry substitutes a well-documented public-domain Know-Nothing cartoon of the same years from the Library of Congress. The speech-balloon text is reproduced from the Library of Congress catalog description of the print (a public-domain federal transcription), keeping its spellings (“dont,” “your'e,” “determind”) as they appear on the print; it should be checked against the image. CAUTION: This is an image made to persuade; read it as the argument of a commercial printmaker selling to a nativist market, not as a description of Catholic intentions.
Document Text
Speech balloons (as transcribed in the Library of Congress catalog record)
Pope: “My friend we have concluded to take charge of your spiritual welfare, and your temporal estate, so that you need not be troubled with the care of them in future; we will say your prayers and spend your money, while you live, and bury you in the Potters Field, when you die. Kneel then! and kiss our big toe in token of submission.”
Brother Jonathan: “No you dont, Mr. Pope! your'e altogether too willing; but you cant put 'the mark of the Beast' on Americans.”
Young America: “You can neither coax, nor frighten our boys, Sir! we can take care of our own worldly affairs, and are determind to "Know nothing" but this book, to guide us in spiritual things.”
First bishop: “I cannot bear to see that boy, with that horrible book.”
Second bishop: “Only let us get a good foot hold on the soil, and we'll burn up those Books and elevate this Country to the Same degree of happiness and prosperity, to which we have brought Italy, Spain, Ireland and many other lands.”
Third bishop: “Sovereign Pontiff! say that if his friends, have any money, when he dies; they may purchase a hole, for him in my cemetery, at a fair price.”
Fourth bishop: “Go ahead Reverend Father; I'll hold our boat by this sprig of shamrock.”
