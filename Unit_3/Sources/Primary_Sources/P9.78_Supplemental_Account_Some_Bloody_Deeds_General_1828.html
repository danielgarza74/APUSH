Anonymous pro-Jackson satirist, signed with the name of Representative John Taliaferro · 1828 (broadside; a manuscript note attributes the printing to the office of the United States’ Telegraph)
 
TOC FIT Chapter 9 · Essay prompt 9.5 (Why Jackson Won in 1828)
WHY HERE A broadside that looks like one more anti-Jackson coffin handbill but turns out to be a parody, piling up absurd “eyewitness” atrocities until Jackson swallows six coffins whole. Students can weigh how both camps in 1828 fought over Jackson’s character and image, and how the Jacksonians answered attack propaganda with ridicule rather than argument.
TREATMENT Public-domain text; excerpt from the Library of Congress transcription
Historical Context

In the 1828 campaign, Adams supporters attacked Andrew Jackson’s fitness for office by recalling the violence of his military career. The Philadelphia editor John Binns issued the best-known example, the “Coffin Handbill,” which used rows of black coffins to stand for six Tennessee militiamen executed for desertion in 1815 on orders confirmed by Jackson, and printed a mournful poem about them. Jackson’s managers answered through a network of friendly newspapers, led in Washington by Duff Green’s United States’ Telegraph.

This broadside copies the coffin format but mocks it. Its narrator claims to be a second “eye witness” and escalates the charges into the ridiculous. It is signed with the name of John Taliaferro, an anti-Jackson congressman from Virginia’s Northern Neck who had denounced Jackson in a speech to his constituents; the closing lines turn his own words against him. The parody shows how far the 1828 contest had become a battle over a candidate’s persona rather than his policies.

Editorial note. The Library of Congress catalogs this broadside under John Taliaferro’s name, but its content shows it is a pro-Jackson parody of the “Coffin Handbill,” not a genuine anti-Jackson supplement; Taliaferro’s name at the foot is part of the joke. A handwritten note on the Library’s copy reads “Printed at the office of the U.S. Telegraph,” Duff Green’s pro-Jackson paper. This reader reproduces the opening four paragraphs and the closing paragraph and signature from the Library of Congress’s American Memory transcription (1999–2001), which states a target accuracy of 99.95 percent; the transcription’s spellings (including “Russel,” “he displeased,” and “Neek”) are kept. Omitted are four middle paragraphs describing further invented “atrocities,” including an affray in Nashville. The six coffins at the head of the sheet carry the initials J. W., D. M., J. H., H. L., D. H., and E. L. CAUTION: This is satire. Nothing in it is a factual account of the 1815 executions; read it as evidence of campaign technique.
Document Text
[SUPPLEMENTAL] ACCOUNT OF SOME OF THE BLOODY DEEDS OF GENERAL JACKSON, BEING A SUPPLEMENT TO THE “COFFIN HANDBILL.”

The writer of the following detail was also “an eye witness” of what he relates, as well as of all that was given to the public sometime ago by another “eye-witness,” in what is generally known by the name of the “Coffin Handbill.”—Yes, fellow citizens, I was an “eye witness” of all I am about to relate to you; and a great deal more, if my memory at this distant period would only serve me; but many incidents of that bloody time, have been effaced from it. The greatest matter of surprise is, that the other “Eye-witness” should have omitted so many of the most atrocious and unnatural acts of Gen. Jackson, which he must assuredly have seen, unless he intentionally closed his eyes. I can only account for this omission by supposing that these deeds of “the sanguinary chieftain” were so horribly atrocious, that the other “eye-witness” feared if he disclosed them, his statement would not be credited by the public. Be this as it may, I am determined to write, and spare not. Now, therefore, my countrymen, prepare yourselves for a recital that must make the blood of the most obdurate curdle; that must make every man's hair stand on end; a recital at which the stoutest heart must sicken; and the very contemplation of which must make human nature shudder at her own depravity!!!

On the morning that the Six Militiamen and their companions determined to take leave of the tented field for their homes, I saw Harris, the preacher, enter the store house belonging to the Quarter Master's Department, and deal out provisions to himself and all those who had determined to go home with him; and he thought “he was doing what was lawful,” as he certainly was, for what is there unlawful in a man's providing himself with the necessary supplies, when he is about to take a long journey?

Col. Russell commanded at the execution of these poor fellows. When they were about to be launched into eternity, Harris addressed the Colonel in the following words: “You know Colonel that I am an honest man. Although I borrowed three months and three days time from the United States, I intended to pay them honestly; for you know, Colonel, that a man is very able to pay this small debt of time, who has many long years of his life yet to come, from which he can take it.” “Yes, Harris,” said the Colonel, “I know your intentions were honest, and am sorry that you are about to suffer so unjustly; and moreover, it is a singular mode that the United States takes to recover their debts, by depriving the debtor of the whole fund from which she can ever look for payment.”—The “gallant Lewis” next addressed the Colonel, and asked him to suspend the execution for a few minutes; when all six of these unfortunate men, kneeling on their respective coffins, commenced singing in the most pathetic manner the sixteen beautiful but mournful verses which are to be found in the middle of the “Coffin Handbill,” Harris, the preacher, giving them out, one line at a time.—When this sad song of death was ended, (the melancholy notes of which still vibrate on my ear,) the fatal word was given, and Six American Militiamen fell to the ground, “shot to death.” I heard the six men who were detailed to fire at the “gallant Lewis,” swear that they intended, and thought that they had pointed their muskets in a different direction, but their eyes being filled with tears, so wonderful are the laws of reflection and refraction, that they afterwards ascertained, by the result, that four of their bullets had penetrated his body. “An eye witness appeals to Col. Russel, who, he thinks, now lives in Alabama, for the perfect truth of this sketch. He does not fear but the Colonel will keenly recollect, and faithfully depict the horrors of the day on which Six Americans were shot to death under his command, but not by his orders.”

Now, “gentle reader,” prepare yourself to receive a shock, which I fear will prove more than you can bear! Shortly after these unfortunate American Militiamen had been consigned to the earth, a peremptory order arrived from the monster Jackson, directing Col Pipkin to have them sent off immediately to him at New Orleans, nailed up in their coffins, or incur the penalty of having his head severed from his body, the first time he (Jackson) came in reach of him.—They were accordingly disinterred, and upon their arrival at head quarters—I shudder whilst I relate it! Would you believe it, “gentle reader,” this monster, this more than cannibal, Gen. Andrew Jackson, eat the whole Six Militiamen at one meal!!! Yes, my shuddering countrymen, he swallowed them whole, coffins and all, without the slightest attempt at mastication!!!!!! If you are disposed to doubt this statement, I can refer you to many of the most respectable officers, who were in service with him at New Orleans. And can you, my deluded countrymen, even think of making this horrible anthropophagian monster President of the United States? If you place him at the head of the government, what pledge can you have, that if he should at any time he displeased with his cabinet, that he will not have all four of his secretaries roasted, and eat them for his dinner!!!!

[. . .]

Lastly, my countrymen, let me entreat you to consider what the ablest and wisest of our public servants have told us of this man-eating monster of “obdurate heart.” It is true, I have detailed to you some of the bloody atrocities of which I was an “eye witness,” for the truth of which I have appealed “to Col. Russell, (who, I think, lives in Alabama;”) and to which, no doubt, the pure and patriotic Henry Clay alluded, when he prayed that you might be visited by war, pestilence, and famine, rather than this man should be made your President. But before we part, my deluded countrymen, let me call your attention to the fact, that Jackson has been proven, (by another great and patriotic statesman,) to be “guilty of conduct that would entitle him to a cell in the penitentiary provided he were dealt with according to his merits”—and this was done in an able speech delivered at King George Court House, Virginia, and addressed to his constituents, by that firm and disinterested servant of the people,

JOHN TALIAFERRO, Member of Congress from Northern Neek, Va.
