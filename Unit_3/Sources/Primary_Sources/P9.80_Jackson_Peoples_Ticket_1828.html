Jackson and the People’s Ticket
Ohio Jackson party ticket (Cincinnati area) · 1828
 
TOC FIT Chapter 9 · Essay prompt 9.5 (Why Jackson Won in 1828)
WHY HERE A local ballot that put Jackson’s name and “the People” at the head of a full slate running from governor down to county coroner. Students can see how loyalty to Jackson was used to carry a whole party list, and what that suggests about how the new party organization reached voters.
TREATMENT Public-domain text, complete
ESSAY PROMPTS 9.5
Historical Context

Ohio held its state and county elections in October 1828, a few weeks before the presidential vote. Party tickets were printed slips that voters carried to the polls and deposited as their ballots; there was no official government ballot in 1828. A party ticket that listed every office let a voter support an entire slate at once, which made the printed ticket one of the main tools of party discipline.

This ticket heads its list with “Jackson and the People’s Ticket,” although Jackson was not on the October ballot. It names John W. Campbell, the Jacksonian candidate for governor, and James Findlay for Congress. Findlay and Robert T. Lytle were Cincinnati men, which suggests the ticket was printed for Hamilton County. Using Jackson’s name to carry local candidates shows how his popularity was being converted into a lasting party.

Editorial note. Reproduced complete from the Library of Congress machine-readable transcription (American Memory, 1999) of the ticket in the Printed Ephemera Collection (Portfolio 92, Folder 11). The ticket is undated and gives no place; the Library dates it 1828, and the Hamilton County identification rests on the candidates’ names and should be treated as probable. The Norton Source Collection does not include this item.

JACKSON AND THE PEOPLE'S TICKET.

Governor, John W. Campbell.

Congress, James Findlay.

Senator, Jonathan Cilley.

Representatives,

Alexander Duncan,

Elijah Hayward,

Robert T. Lytle

Sheriff, John C. Avery.

Commissioner, Leonard Armstrong.

Auditor, John T. Jones.

Coroner, David Jackson, jr.
