Field Detail
TOC Fit Chapter 10 · Essay prompt 10.4 (The Bank War and the Panic of 1837)
Why Here A pro-Jackson print stages the Bank War as a prizefight that Jackson wins against Nicholas Biddle, backed by “Mother Bank” and her money. Students can read it as an argument by its makers about who stood on each side of the Bank War, and set its picture of the Bank as a bought-and-paid-for interest against opposition prints of Jackson as a tyrant.
Treatment Public-domain image; speech balloons and letterpress as transcribed in the repository record
Historical Context
After Jackson removed federal deposits from the Second Bank of the United States in the fall of 1833, the Bank’s president, Nicholas Biddle, sharply cut back the Bank’s loans. He argued the Bank had to protect itself after losing the government’s money, but he also hoped the resulting credit squeeze would pressure Congress to restore the deposits and recharter the Bank. Businesses failed and petitions poured into Congress during the winter of 1833–34, the so-called Panic Session.
New York lithographer Anthony Imbert deposited this print for copyright on March 5, 1834. It borrows the language and setting of popular bare-knuckle boxing (“the fancy” were boxing fans and gamblers). Henry Clay and Daniel Webster, leaders of the Senate opposition, stand in Biddle’s corner; Vice President Martin Van Buren, the comic Yankee character Major Jack Downing, and a frontiersman representing New York’s Tammany Democrats stand in Jackson’s.

Document Text
Speech balloons
Mother Bank: “Darken his day lights, Nick Put the Screws to him my tulip!”
Webster: “Blow me tight if Nick ain't been crammed too much You see as how he's losing his wind!”
Third figure at left: “Hurrah Nick my kiddy! hit him a polt in the Smellers!”
Figure behind Jackson: “Go it Hickory, my old buffer! give it to him in the bread basket, it will make him throw up his deposits!”
Downing: “I swan if the Ginral hain't been taken lessons from Fuller!”
Tammany: “Hurrah my old yallow flower of the forrest, walk into him like a streak of Greased lightning through a gooseberry bush!”
Bottle on the ground: “Old Monongohala Whiskey.”
Letterpress below the image
This celebrated fight took place at Washington in 1834, Hickory was seconded by Little Van and Major Jack Downing, with Joe Tammany for bottle holder; Long Harry and Black Dan were Nick's seconds, and Old Mother Bank bottle holder.
Several long and severe rounds were fought, and from the immense sums bet, many of the fancy were losers to a large amount Old Mother B. is said to have backed her champion to the tune of more than $150,000. Nick's weight of metal was superior as well as his science, but neither were sufficient for the pluck and wind of Hickory, who shewed his thorough training and sound condition so effectually that in the last round Nick was unable to come to time and gave in.
