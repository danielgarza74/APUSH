The Grand National Caravan Moving East
“Hassan Straightshanks” (probably David Claypoole Johnston), artist; Endicott & Swett, publisher (New York) · 1833 (lithograph)
 
Field Detail
TOC Fit Chapter 10 · Essay prompt 10.5 (Judging Jackson's Presidency)
Why Here An opposition print turns Jackson’s 1833 tour of the Northeast into a mock royal procession, with Van Buren riding behind him, a devil fiddling, and a caged Indian in tow. Students can read it as an argument by its maker that Jackson’s popularity was hollow pageantry, and test its charges against the songs and banners it puts in the mouths of his followers.
Treatment Public-domain image; song lines, banners, and caption as transcribed in the repository record
Historical Context
In June 1833 Jackson made a triumphal tour of the Northeast, drawing large crowds in Baltimore, Philadelphia, New York, and Boston, where Harvard gave him an honorary degree. Vice President Martin Van Buren traveled with him, and opponents read the trip as a campaign to groom Van Buren as Jackson’s successor. At the same time, the Sauk leader Black Hawk, captured after the Black Hawk War of 1832, was being taken through eastern cities as a prisoner of war; he reached New York in the same week as the president.
The print, entered for copyright in New York in 1833, is signed with a joke name and credited to the supervision of “Maj. Jack Downing,” a fictional Yankee commentator whose letters were popular newspaper satire. Its Biblical caption mocks the procession as something never seen before or since, and each figure sings a line from a well-known song.
Editorial note. The text is reproduced from the Library of Congress catalog description of the print (a public-domain federal transcription) and should be checked against the image. The catalog gives the Biblical caption in two versions, once with “hath not been” and once with “hath been”; this reader follows the first, which matches the wording of Joel 2:2, but the image should decide. The Library of Congress suggests the caged figure refers to Black Hawk and attributes the drawing to David Claypoole Johnston, possibly with John Henry Bufford as printer; both are attributions, not certainties. CAUTION: This is an image made to persuade. Read it as the argument of Jackson’s opponents, not as a report of the tour. The caption and song lines were checked against the Library of Congress master image, which reads “hath not been”; four banner and ground inscriptions not in the catalog record were added from the image. Document Text
Caption below the title
“There hath not been the like of them, neither shall there be any more after them, even to the years of many generations.”
Song lines sung by the figures
Jackson: “I've kissed and I've prattled to fifty fair maids.”
Van Buren: “Had I a heart for falshood fram'd.”
Devil: “When wild wars deadly blast was blown.”
Soldier: “How happy's [the] soldier that lives on his pay.”
Caged Indian: “Home! Sweet home!”
Monkey atop the wagon: “Merrily every bosom boundeth.”
Drunk lying in the foreground: “Hail! Columbia, happy land.”
Flags and banners
Wagon: “Rights of Man.”
“See the Conquering Hero comes / Sound the trumpet beat the drums.”
“Remember the glories of Brien the Brave.”
“Honour and gratitude to the man who has filled the measure of his countrys glory.”
Atop the hickory tree: “Heaven send it happy dew, / Earth lend it sap anew / Gaily to burgeon / And broadly to grow.”
Balloon overhead: “rising generation.”
Banner: “Home, Love and Liberty.”
Banner: “The Conqu[illegible] of Hearts.”
Banner: “None but the Brave deserve the Fair.”
Beneath the lead horses: “Thus strew the way with Flowers.”
