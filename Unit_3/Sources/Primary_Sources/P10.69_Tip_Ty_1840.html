Tip and Ty: A New Comic Whig Glee (sheet music)
Unattributed (words usually credited to Alexander Coffman Ross) · Boston: Parker & Ditson, c. 1840
 
TOC FIT Chapter 10 · Essay prompt 10.1 (How Democratic Was Jacksonian Democracy?); Essay prompt 10.5 (Judging Jackson's Presidency)
WHY HERE The Whigs’ best-known campaign song turned the 1840 election into a singing, marching movement, mocking Van Buren as “a used up man” and embracing the log cabin and hard cider that Democrats had sneered at. Students can weigh it as evidence that mass, popular party politics had fully arrived, and that the Whigs had learned the Jacksonians’ methods.
TREATMENT Public-domain image; song text transcribed from the Boston sheet-music edition (Cornell copy)
Historical Context

In 1840 the Whig Party nominated William Henry Harrison, a sixty-seven-year-old general famous for the Battle of Tippecanoe (1811), with John Tyler of Virginia as his running mate. When a Democratic paper sneered that Harrison would be content with a pension, a log cabin, and a barrel of hard cider, Whigs adopted the cabin and the cider as symbols of a plain man of the people, even though Harrison came from a wealthy Virginia planter family and lived in a large house in Ohio.

Whigs organized parades, rallies, and huge conventions such as the Bunker Hill gathering in Boston in September 1840, to which this edition is dedicated. Songbooks and sheet music spread the campaign’s slogans; “Tippecanoe and Tyler too,” first sung in Ohio, became the most famous. Turnout rose to about 80 percent of eligible voters, and Harrison defeated Van Buren in the depths of the depression that followed the Panic of 1837.

Editorial note. This entry presents the Boston sheet-music edition from the copies at Cornell University Library and the Lester S. Levy Sheet Music Collection, Johns Hopkins University. The first leaf of Cornell’s scan is blank, so the image reproduced is the second leaf, which carries the title, dedication, and music; the further stanzas are transcribed from the third leaf of the same scan. The Boston edition’s text replaces the Ohio songbook version (The Harrison and Log Cabin Song Book, Columbus, 1840) used in an earlier draft; the two differ in wording, and the Boston edition has its own Massachusetts stanza. CAUTION: Campaign songs are partisan entertainment; the image of Harrison as a humble cabin-dweller was a deliberate construction. Document Text
Transcription of the printed text

“TIP AND TY.” A new Comic Whig Glee. Respectfully dedicated to the Louisiana Whig Delegation to the Bunker Hill Convention. Boston. Published by Parker & Ditson, Washington Street.

1 (words printed under the music)

O what has caused this great commotion, motion, motion,
Our country through,
It is the ball that’s rolling on,
For Tippecanoe and Tyler too,
For Tippecanoe and Tyler too;
And with them we’ll beat little Van, Van,
Van, Van, oh he’s a used up man,
And with them we’ll beat little Van.

Further stanzas (printed on the following page)

2. Like the working of mighty waters, waters, waters,
On it will go,
And in its course will clear the way
For Tippecanoe and Tyler too, &c.

3. See the Loco’s standard tottering, tottering, tottering,
Down it must go,
And in its place we’ll rear the flag,
Of Tippecanoe and Tyler too, &c.

4. The Bay State boys turned out in thousands, thousands, thousands,
Not long ago,
And at Bunker Hill, they set their seals
For Tippecanoe and Tyler too, &c.

5. Now you hear the Vanjacks talking, talking, talking,
Things look quite blue
For all the world seems turning round
For Tippecanoe and Tyler too, &c.

6. Let them talk about hard cider, cider, cider,
And Log Cabins too,
It will only help to speed the ball,
For Tippecanoe and Tyler too, &c.

7. His latchstring hangs outside the door, door, door,
And is never pulled in,
For it always was the custom of
Old Tippecanoe and Tyler too, &c.

8. He always has his tables set, set, set,
For all honest and true,
To ask you in to take a bite,
With Tippecanoe and Tyler too, &c.

9. See the spoilsmen and leg treasurers, treasurers, treasurers,
All in a stew,
For well they know they stand no chance,
With Tippecanoe and Tyler too, &c.

10. Little Matty’s days are numbered, numbered, numbered,
And out he must go,
For in his place, we’ll put the good
Old Tippecanoe and Tyler too, &c.
