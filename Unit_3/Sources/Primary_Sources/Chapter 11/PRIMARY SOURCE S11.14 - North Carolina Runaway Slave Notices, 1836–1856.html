Field Detail
TOC Fit Chapter 11 · Essay prompt 11.3 (The Nature of Slavery); Essay prompt 11.4 (Forms of Resistance)
Why Here Four reward notices placed by enslavers in North Carolina newspapers describe people who fled: a married couple heading for Virginia, a man hiding near his relatives, a husband and wife thought to be lurking near neighboring plantations, and a skilled harness maker who had passed as white for three years. Students can use the details enslavers were forced to record to weigh how common, how planned, and how family-centered flight was.
Treatment Public-domain texts, complete notices
Historical Context
Enslavers who wanted to recover people who had run away often paid to print notices in local newspapers. A notice usually described the person’s age, height, skin color, clothing, scars, skills, and manner of speaking, named the reward, and guessed where the person had gone. Because enslavers wrote them to get people back, the notices reveal what enslavers knew, or believed, about the people they claimed as property, including family ties, trades, literacy, and networks of friends who might hide them.
The North Carolina Runaway Slave Notices project at the University of North Carolina at Greensboro, with North Carolina A&T State University, has collected and transcribed more than 5,000 notices published in the state’s newspapers between 1751 and 1865. Many notices were reprinted for weeks or months, and many were never answered: some of the people described were captured, but others reached the North, joined maroon communities in swamps, or lived for years hidden near their families.
Editorial note. This reader reproduces four complete notices, dated 1836–1856, as transcribed by the North Carolina Runaway Slave Notices project; each notice’s database identifier is given in its heading. The project’s transcriptions keep the spelling and punctuation of the printed notices; “[sic]” and “[?]” are the transcribers’ marks. The newspapers’ printing codes at the end of some notices (e.g., “82-tf.”) and instructions to other newspapers are part of the original advertisements. The database states that its images and materials are for personal, educational, or research use; the notices themselves were published before 1861 and are in the public domain, and only their texts are reproduced here. CAUTION: These are enslavers’ descriptions, written to recapture people; the motives, plans, and inner lives of the people who escaped appear only through their owners’ guesses.
Document Text
“Stop the Runaways!” Raleigh Register and North Carolina Weekly Advertiser, August 16, 1836 (NC.WAK.124.18360816.3.1800)
STOP THE RUNAWAYS! RUNAWAY from the Subscriber, living 14 miles North of Raleigh, on Friday night the 12th instant, his negro boy ABRAHAM and his wife GRACE.-- The man is 27 years of age, about 6 feet high, well proportioned, with tolerably thick lips, and the woman is about 36--the former not very dark complected--the later, tall, this visaged, and quite dark. Abraham had no marks that I recollect of save that on the day of the election he got into a fight in Raleigh; and received a severe blow over the left eye, the effects of which will probably remain sometime. He is remarkable too, for a bold, impudent, swaggering air, which will attract the attention of everyone. The woman has a notable letter[?] or tumor on one of her hands, which, if examined, cannot fail to lead to her being identified. They will aim for Petersburg, I think, the boy having been for years engaged in the wagoning business to and from that place. I will give FIFTY DOLLARS reward for the apprehension of Abraham and TEN DOLLARS for his wife, if taken in the State, or double that amount, if taken out of the State. DAVID GILL. Wake Co. August 15. 39 The Star, and Petersburg Intelligencer will insert the foregoing for six weeks, and send their bills to this Office.
“$100 Reward.” Wilmington Chronicle, December 9, 1840 (NC.NEW.167.18401209.3.3760)
$100 Reward. RANAWAY six weeks since, a negro man named JOHN, commonly known among the negroes as JOHN COOK, who worked for some time under Mr. Hinton James, on the river. Said fellow is about forty years of age, five feet 8 or 9 inches high, spare and slim made, very black complexion, with a wide space between his two upper front teeth, wears whiskers a small round spot on the crown of his head bald, has the appearence as if the hair was worn off, a few grey hairs in his whiskers. Said fellow was taken up a short time since in town, and released upon his promise of coming home; a letter was put in his charge to me by one of the persons who took him, and I have reason to believe he may be passing about upon the credit of said letter. He is frequently in town, as I am informed, about the Rail Road Depot and lower Saw Mills, and also in the neighborhood of Forceput and Mr. Thomas I. Davis' on the North East river, where he has relations. The above reward will be given to any one who will bring proof sufficient to convict of his being harboured or employed by any person, or FIFTY DOLLARS for his delivery to me, or his confinement in any way so that I can get him. J. H. WATTERS. Dec.9 1840. 82-tf.
“$100 Reward!!” Newbernian and North Carolina Advocate (New Bern), May 7, 1849 (NC.CRA.88.18490507.2.3900)
$100 REWARD!! RANAWAY from the subscriber in Jones county, near Beaver Creek, on Wednesday the 25th of April last, a negro man named MINGO, and his wife, ANACA, or ANNA. Said negro man is about 35 years of age, of large size, over six feet in height, square built, and weighs from 180 to 190 pounds; has lost a portion of his upper, and under teeth, and when walking throws his toes well out, throws his head well back, and has a quick step. Anaca is about 27 or 28 years of age, over middle height, thick set, kidney foot, and when walking turns her toes well out. She speaks quick, and has rather a down look. Said negroes are probably lurking about the plantation of John Foscue, in Jones county, or on Core Creek, near Dr. Hubbard's, in Craven county, or in the neighborhood of the Messrs. Rheins, on the Neuse. The above reward will be given to any person who will deliver said negroes to me, at my residence, or confine them in any jail in the State, so I get them. For the delivery or confinement of the negro man alone, as above named, I will give $75.-'Masters of vessels are particularly cautioned not to aid said negroes to escape. ENOCH NOBLE. Jones county, May 7, 1849.
“$400 Reward.” Asheville News, June 5, 1856 (NC.BUN.8.18560605.3.3060)
$400 REWARD. SOME three years since my negro man, Amos, absconded, since which time I have been unable to get hold of him. He is about 5 feet 7 to 10 inches high, about twenty eight years old, nearly white, his face freckled, hair inclined to curl at the bottom of his head, eyes hazle [sic] or blue. He is a good HARNESS maker, somewhat dissipated in his habits, can read, write and cypher a little, and is no doubt passing himself for a white man. Amos was raised by Mr. Lewis Dickey, of Spartanburg District, and his wife belongs to Dr. Bivings of Spartanburg. The last certain information I had of him he was in Kanawha county, Va. I believe he is in Western North Carolina or Western Virginia. I will give Four Hundred Dollars for his confinement in any Jail, so that I get him again. W. G. NEEL. Harrisburg, Abbevile [sic] Dist., S. C., June 5, 1856.
