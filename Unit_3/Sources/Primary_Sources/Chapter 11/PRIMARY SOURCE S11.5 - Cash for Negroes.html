“Cash for Negroes” (slave trader’s newspaper advertisement)
Unsigned (“the subscriber,” attributed to John Armfield) · June 5, 1826 (Alexandria Phenix Gazette)
 
Field Detail
TOC Fit Chapter 11 · Essay prompt 11.1 (Slavery and Capitalism); Essay prompt 11.3 (The Nature of Slavery)
Why Here A short notice offering the “highest price” for thirty-five or forty enslaved people, bought singly or as families. Students can read it as evidence that buying people for resale was advertised and conducted as an ordinary, cash-driven business in the Upper South.
Treatment Public-domain text; complete
Historical Context
After Congress closed the Atlantic slave trade in 1808, the spread of cotton and sugar planting into Alabama, Mississippi, and Louisiana created a large demand for enslaved workers, while planters in Virginia and Maryland, whose tobacco lands were declining, held more people than they needed. Professional traders moved roughly a million people from the Upper South to the Lower South between 1790 and 1860, about two-thirds of them through sales.
Advertisements like this one ran week after week in Upper South newspapers. Traders offered cash, named a tavern or boardinghouse where sellers could find them, and used the word “likely” to mean young, healthy, and saleable. In 1828 John Armfield formed a partnership with Isaac Franklin; their Alexandria firm, Franklin and Armfield, became the largest slave-trading business in the country.
Editorial note. Reproduced complete from the transcription published by Encyclopedia Virginia (Virginia Humanities), which identifies its source as the Alexandria Phenix Gazette, June 5, 1826, p. 3 (Library of Congress, Chronicling America). Two slips in that transcription have been corrected (“Legg’s ,” and “St. Asaphstreet”); the newspaper page image should be checked to confirm them. Capitals follow the transcription. The advertisement is unsigned; its attribution to John Armfield, as his first advertisement, is the Encyclopedia Virginia editors’.
Document Text
Cash for Negroes.
THE subscriber wishes to purchase THIRTY-FIVE OR FORTY LIKELY NEGROES, either separately or in families, for which the highest price will be given. –Apply at Mr. Elias P. Legg’s, on St. Asaph street, or any information addressed to me in Alexandria, through the post-office, will be attended to.
