Historical Context
In Cohens v. Virginia (March 1821) two brothers convicted in Norfolk of selling lottery tickets appealed to the U.S. Supreme Court. Marshall’s opinion ruled against the Cohens on the merits, but it held that the Court could review the judgments of state courts, even in criminal cases where a state was a party. Virginia Republicans, who had already fought Martin v. Hunter’s Lessee (1816) and McCulloch v. Maryland (1819), saw the decision as a final blow to state sovereignty.
Madison had answered Roane’s April plea privately on May 6, 1821, sharing his own doubts about the Court’s reasoning but declining to enter the newspaper fight. Roane then wrote five essays of his own, signed “Algernon Sidney,” which appeared in the Richmond Enquirer between May 25 and June 8, 1821. Marshall read them and complained privately that their author was the “champion of dismemberment.”
Editorial note. This reader reproduces the letter from Founders Online (National Archives), which prints it from The Papers of James Madison, Retirement Series, vol. 2, 1 February 1820–26 February 1823 (pp. 339–40). Spelling, capitalization, and punctuation are as transcribed there; the modern editors’ annotations and note numbers are not reproduced. Roane’s “interesting favour” is Madison’s letter to him of May 6, 1821; the “Enclosed Numbers” are the five “Algernon Sidney” essays, published in the Richmond Enquirer on May 25 and 29 and June 1, 5, and 8, 1821.
Document Text
Richmond, June 20th. 1821 Dear Sir
I had the honor to receive, some short time since, your interesting favour, on the subject which is discussed in the Enclosed Numbers. I found it extreemly able, and satisfactory, and I return you my thanks for the favour. The Enclosed No’s were written by me, just before, and have been published in the Enquirer. I had hoped to have had a few Copies struck in a more Eligible form, to be presented to my particular, and my distinguished friends, but have been disappointed. I now doubt whether I ought to venture to send them to you, as taken from the Columns of a news paper. Yet the subject is important; and I am desirous of placing them before the Eyes of the distinguished citizen, who, more than any other, contributed to found our excellent constitution. With great consideration, respect, and Esteem, I am, dear sir, Yr: obt: Servant
Spencer Roane
