“To Sweep the Augean Stable”: Jackson and Calhoun Electoral Ticket (Ohio)
Ohio Jackson electoral ticket · 1828 (printed slip with woodcut of a broom)
 
Field Detail
TOC Fit Chapter 9 · Essay prompt 9.5 (Why Jackson Won in 1828)
Why Here A ballot that Ohio Jacksonians handed to voters, headed by a broom and a classical slogan about cleaning out a filthy stable. Students can read how a single image and motto turned the charge of corruption in Washington into a reason to vote, and how a statewide slate of electors tied local men to the national ticket.
Treatment Public-domain text, complete; image of the ticket
Historical Context
In the Greek myth, Hercules cleaned the stables of King Augeas, unwashed for thirty years, in a single day by turning two rivers through them. Jackson’s supporters used the story to promise that he would purge a government they described as corrupted by the “bargain” of 1825 and by Adams’s patronage. The broom became one of the campaign’s best-known emblems.
Ohio chose its sixteen presidential electors by statewide popular vote. Party tickets were printed slips that voters carried to the polls and deposited as their ballots; there was no official government ballot in 1828. This ticket lists the Jackson-Calhoun electors, each identified by county so that voters across the state could recognize local names. Jackson carried Ohio in November 1828 by a narrow margin.
Editorial note. Reproduced complete from the Library of Congress machine-readable transcription (American Memory, 1999) of the ticket in the Printed Ephemera Collection; the transcription renders the woodcut of a broom as an image note, and a later handwritten accession number (“Ac. 421.”) is omitted. Spelling and punctuation (including “HUGH M' FALL” and the inconsistent end punctuation) follow the transcription. The ticket is undated; the Library dates it 1828. The image record below should be captured to show the broom cut. The Norton Source Collection does not include this item.
