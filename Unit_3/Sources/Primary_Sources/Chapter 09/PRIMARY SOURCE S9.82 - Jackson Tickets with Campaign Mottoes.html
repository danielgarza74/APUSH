Jackson Tickets with Campaign Mottoes (Baltimore)
Unidentified printer, probably Baltimore · 1828 (five election tickets with woodcuts)
 
Field Detail
TOC Fit Chapter 9 · Essay prompt 9.5 (Why Jackson Won in 1828)
Why Here Five small ballots for the same two Jacksonian candidates, each with a different picture and slogan, from Jackson’s portrait and a hickory tree to a locomotive and the “American System.” Students can compare the designs to see how Jackson’s managers fitted symbols to different groups of voters, and how vague the campaign could be about policy.
Treatment Public-domain images; mottoes from the repository’s catalog titles; candidate names to be transcribed from the images
Historical Context
In 1828 Baltimore’s Jacksonians ran John Van Lear McMahon and George H. Steuart for the city’s two seats in the Maryland House of Delegates. Party tickets were printed slips that voters carried to the polls and deposited as their ballots; there was no official government ballot. The Library of Congress holds a series of these tickets for the two candidates, each with its own woodcut and motto.
The mottoes range widely. One quotes Jefferson’s praise of Jackson, and one rallies voters around the hickory tree, symbol of “Old Hickory.” Others promise “Internal improvement by rail roads, canals, &c.,” honor “Agriculture, commerce and manufactures,” and even claim the “American System,” the program of tariffs and internal improvements associated with Henry Clay and the Adams administration. In a commercial and manufacturing city, Jacksonians competed for voters who favored those policies, while in other regions the party played them down.
Editorial note. Images from the Library of Congress Rare Book and Special Collections Division (Broadside Collection, portfolio 192, no. 12), digitized from black-and-white film copy negatives; the Library states “No known restrictions on publication.” The Library’s catalog (following Bernard F. Reilly, American Political Prints, 1766–1876, entries 1828-5 through 1828-10) identifies the series as tickets for McMahon and Steuart, probably printed in Baltimore. The mottoes given as component headings are the Library’s catalog transcriptions of each ticket’s title; the candidates’ names and any other printed text on the tickets must be transcribed from the images. One ticket in Reilly’s series (1828-6) was not part of the plan and is not included. The planner’s guess that the tickets were tailored to different regions is refined here: all five were for the same Baltimore race. The Norton Source Collection does not include these tickets.
