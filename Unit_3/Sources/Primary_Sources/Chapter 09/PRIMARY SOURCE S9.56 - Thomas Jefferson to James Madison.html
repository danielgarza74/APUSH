Historical Context
On October 17, 1823, President James Monroe sent Thomas Jefferson the letters in which British foreign secretary George Canning proposed a joint Anglo-American declaration against European intervention in Spanish America, and asked Jefferson to pass them on to James Madison. On October 24 Jefferson wrote his long answer to Monroe and, the same day, sent the papers on to Madison at Montpelier with this short note.
Jefferson and Madison, both retired Virginia ex-presidents and neighbors in the Piedmont, corresponded constantly on public and private matters. The rest of the note turns to everyday business between them: the price of John Trumbull’s engraving of the signing of the Declaration of Independence and an answer to a question about the history of astronomy. Madison replied to Monroe directly on October 30.
Editorial note. Reproduced from the transcription published on Founders Online (National Archives), from The Papers of Thomas Jefferson, Retirement Series, vol. 20 (2023), p. 265. Jefferson’s spelling and lowercase sentence openings are kept as written; “Decln” is his abbreviation for “Declaration.” The modern editors’ notes are not reproduced. CAUTION: This note shows only that the papers were forwarded; Jefferson’s actual advice is in his letter to Monroe of the same date, and Madison’s is in his letter to Monroe of October 30, 1823.
Document Text
Monticello Oct. 24. 23.
Th:J. to J. Madison.
I forward you two most important letters sent to me by the President and add his letter to me by which you will percieve his primâ facie views. this you will be so good as to return to me, and forward the others to him. I have recieved Trumbull’s print of the Decln of Independance, & turning to his letter am able to inform you more certainly than I could by memory that the print costs 20.D. & the frame & glass 12.D. say 32.D. in all.
to answer your question, Pythagoras has the reputation of having first taught the true position of the sun in the center of our system & the revolution of the planets around it. his doctrine, after a long eclipse was restored by Copernicus, and hence it is called either the Pythagorean or Copernican system. health and affectionate salutations to mrs Madison and yourself
