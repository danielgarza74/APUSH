Jackson Ticket (Virginia)
Virginia Jackson electoral ticket · 1828
 
Field Detail
TOC Fit Chapter 9 · Essay prompt 9.5 (Why Jackson Won in 1828)
Why Here A statewide ballot listing Virginia’s twenty-four Jackson electors, one for each district, many identified by militia or professional titles. Students can use it to see how a party organized across a large state and chose locally respected men to carry Jackson’s name.
Treatment Public-domain text, complete
Historical Context
Virginia chose its twenty-four presidential electors on a statewide general ticket, so every voter needed a printed list of the whole slate. Party tickets were printed slips that voters carried to the polls and deposited as their ballots; there was no official government ballot in 1828. Jackson’s Virginia campaign was directed by a central committee in Richmond working closely with Thomas Ritchie’s Richmond Enquirer, part of the alliance Martin Van Buren built between New York Republicans and the Virginia leadership.
The electors were drawn from every region of the state, from Norfolk and the Eastern Shore to the Shenandoah Valley and the trans-Allegheny west, and many carry titles such as Colonel, Captain, Judge, or Doctor. Jackson carried Virginia decisively in November 1828.
Editorial note. Reproduced complete from the Library of Congress machine-readable transcription (American Memory, 1999) of the ticket in the Printed Ephemera Collection (Portfolio 186, Folder 13a). The transcription also includes a twentieth-century autograph dealer’s description and label (“The American Autograph Shop, … Ridley Park, Pennsylvania”) on the other pages of the item; these are not part of the 1828 ticket and are omitted. Spellings such as “Nolloway” (for Nottoway) and “Rockinghum” are kept as transcribed. The ticket is undated; the Library dates it to 1828. The Norton Source Collection does not include this item.
