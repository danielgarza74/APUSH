Selection Overview

Warshauer argues that Jackson, who owned upwards of one hundred and fifty slaves and never questioned slavery's morality, epitomized southern paternalism as a slave master. He saw his slaves as his black "family," provided medical care and hired top lawyers to save slaves on trial for their lives, yet he also used slaves to settle debts and offered money for punishing runaways.
