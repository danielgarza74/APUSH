THE SCOPE OF THE SECOND GREAT AWAKENING: ANDOVER, MASSACHUSETTS, AS A CASE STUDY Richard D. Shiels The Second Great Awakening was a national phenomenon that began in the late eighteenth century and continued through the first third of the nineteenth. Americans in virtually every town or county experienced religious revivals and organized evangelical voluntary societies. Such a far-reaching, long-lasting, grass-roots movement defies explanation or even definition when considered as a whole.' Consequently, most historians write about one or another part of the awakening: the "new measures" used in revivals, for example, or the New Haven theology; voluntary societies or Methodist camp meetings. Some study short periods of revival rather than the entire period of the awakening, and most focus upon one or another group of participants. Recent works by William G. McLoughlin and Donald Scott focus primarily upon the clergy. McLoughlin is interested in what it was that preachers said which met with such popular response. He argues that social changes resulting from the American Revolu- tion required new ideas of personal and national identity. Both suggest that the key to the awakening is what clergymen did when facing a new situation.2 Several other studies examine the converts in this awaken- ing, seemingly discounting the role of clergy. Paul Johnson finds male shopkeepers at the heart of the revival in Rochester, New York, in 1831. He concludes that the revival was related to a changing relationship between these men and their employees.3 Nancy Cott focuses upon young women in New England's awakening and Mary Ryan describes the role of adult women in the movement in Oneida County, New York, in the 1820s. Cott and Ryan look for its causes in newly emerging sex roles and family structures.4 Randall Roth identifies young male con- verts in frontier communities in Vermont. He and Joseph Kett suggest that the awakening was linked to new circumstances con- fronting youth.5 Although parallel in focusing upon the question "who was converted?" these studies provide different answers and therefore different interpretations of the awakening. This study is limited to the Second Great Awakening in South Parish, Andover, Massachusetts. Its purpose is to discover the scope of the movement within one community over the entire period of the awakening.
