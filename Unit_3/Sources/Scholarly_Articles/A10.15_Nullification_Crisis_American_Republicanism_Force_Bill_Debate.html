Selection Overview

Ericson argues that participants in the 1832-1833 nullification crisis did not see it as a "prelude to civil war" but as part of a long intellectual conflict over the meaning of republicanism. He notes that no other southern state defended South Carolina's ordinances against the tariffs of 1828 and 1832, and that the Compromise Tariff of 1833 defused the clash over Jackson's Force Bill.

ERICSON It seems to me that the subjects embraced by the proceedings of South Carolina afford ample field for the exercise of intellect with intellect .... -Senator George M. Bibb (D-Ky.)1 THE NULLIFICATION CRISIS OF 1832-1833 IS USUALLY TREATED AS ONE of the major sectional conflicts leading up to the Civil War, along with the Missouri crisis of 1819-1820, the crisis of 1850, and, of course, the secession crisis of 1860-1861. This view of the nullification crisis immediately places the crisis within a larger pattern and provides it with a cause and a telos. However, any view that projects a pattern back onto historical events is problematic, especially when the se- quence of events is shrouded in the mantle of inevitability as an irre- pressible conflict.
