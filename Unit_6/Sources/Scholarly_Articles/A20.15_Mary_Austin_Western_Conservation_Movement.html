Selection Overview

Blend portrays Mary Austin as a regional, partly agrarian conservationist who rejected bigness and commercial values and drew on women's municipal housekeeping, while the gospel of efficiency pushed female aesthetic conservationists aside.

Mary Austin <■ 13 Little Rain, she left the desert to join other artists at Carmel. Short- ish and slightly dumpy, and already afflicted by the tragedy of a retarded child and a failing marriage, Austin threw herself into her work. Out of this period came Isidro (1915), a romance of Califor- nia's missions, and The Flock (1916), an account of sheepherding in that state.2 By 1916, Austin was disillusioned with California. "A place of mystery was eaten up," she wrote, "it was made into building lots, cannery sites; it receded before preemptions of rock crushers and city dumps." She had made common cause with her husband against the attempt of Los Angeles to gain control over the Owens Valley water supply, perhaps as much to bolster her marriage and dwin- dling finances as to protect the valley. With the destruction of the area, she fled to Europe in search of a new identity and a new environment. While touring the Continent, she learned about self- healing at a convent in Italy and about British Socialism at meetings of the Fabian Society in London. Upon her return, Austin sought out literary circles in New York before finally settling on New Mex- ico, in 1924, as a permanent residence. Over these years she con- tinued to write prodigiously, including A Woman of Genius (1912), one of three feminist novels; Christ in Italy (1912), a study of mysti- cism; Land of Journey's Ending (1924), a tribute to her newly adopted region; and several works ranging from poetry to collections of folklore.3 In her personal philosophy, she shared the sense of exclusion and individualism which characterized such male writers as George Wharton James, John Van Dyke, Joseph Wood Krutch, and Edward Abbey. On a broader level, she partially fits into Carolyn Merchant's portrait of women who entered the early Progressive movement in America as protectors of the environment and friends of endangered species.
