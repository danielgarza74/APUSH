First-Nighters Posing for the Camera outside the Warners’ Theater before the Premiere of “Don Juan” with John Barrymore
Irving Browning · August 6, 1926
 
TOC FIT The Rise of Mass Culture: A Love Affair with Movies
WHY HERE A crowd of New Yorkers poses under a blazing marquee on the opening night of the first feature film shown with a synchronized Vitaphone sound track.
TREATMENT Public-domain image
Historical Context

Irving Browning, a New York commercial photographer, made this photograph on August 6, 1926, outside the Warners’ Theatre on Broadway in Manhattan, where Warner Bros. was opening Don Juan, a swashbuckling costume picture starring John Barrymore. Don Juan was the first feature film released with the Vitaphone system, which played a recorded orchestral score and sound effects on discs synchronized with the projector. The program also included short sound films of musicians and singers. There was still no spoken dialogue in the feature; talking pictures arrived with The Jazz Singer in October 1927. The photograph later entered the records of the federal Office of War Information, which collected pictures of American life during World War II.

By the mid-1920s tens of millions of Americans went to the movies every week, and studios built large, lavish theaters in big cities to draw them. Notice who came to this opening: men in straw hats, women in short skirts and cloche hats, teenagers, and children. Notice too what the theater advertised besides the film, including its “Refrigerated Washed-Air Cooling System” on a hot August night.
