Scopes
The Crisis (W. E. B. Du Bois, editor) · September 1925
 
TOC FIT The Scopes Trial; Fundamentalism
WHY HERE The NAACP's magazine argues that the Dayton evolution trial was not a backwoods oddity but a sign of a national habit of ignorance that also kept Black Americans undereducated and unprotected.
TREATMENT Public-domain text, complete
Historical Context

The Crisis was the monthly magazine of the National Association for the Advancement of Colored People (NAACP), founded in 1910 and edited from New York by the scholar and activist W. E. B. Du Bois. Its unsigned editorials were generally Du Bois's own work, and the magazine reached a national readership of Black Americans and white sympathizers. This short editorial appeared on page 218 of the September 1925 issue, about six weeks after the trial of John T. Scopes ended in Dayton, Tennessee, with his conviction for teaching evolution in violation of the state's new Butler Act.

Much of the national press had treated the trial as a comic spectacle of rural Southern backwardness. The Crisis rejected that reading. Notice how the editorial shifts blame away from the 'back-woodsmen' of Dayton and onto educated Americans (professors, scientists, ministers, and the wealthy), and how it ties the fight over teaching science in white schools to the denial of education, voting rights, and protection from lynching to Black Southerners. The references to the 18th Amendment (Prohibition) and the 15th Amendment (voting rights regardless of race) point to which constitutional commands white Southern leaders chose to enforce.

Editorial note. The editorial is reproduced complete. The page also carries the end of a preceding item and the start of the next editorial; both are left out.
Document Text
Scopes

One hundred per cent Americans are now endeavoring to persuade hilarious and sarcastic Europe that Dayton, Tennessee, is a huge joke and very, very exceptional. And in proof of all this the learned American press is emitting huge guffaws and peals of Brobdingnagian laughter combined with streaming tears. But few are deceived, even of those who joke and slap each other on the back. The truth is and we know it: Dayton, Tennessee, is America: a great, ignorant, simple-minded land, curiously compounded of brutality, bigotry, religious faith and demagoguery, and capable not simply of mistakes but of persecution, lynching, murder and idiotic blundering, as well as charity, missions, love and hope.

That is America and America is what it is because we believe in Ignorance. The whole modern Nordic civilization of which America is a great and leading branch has sold its soul to Ignorance. Its leading priests profess a religious faith which they do not believe and which they know, and every man of intelligence knows, they do not and cannot believe; and then when a knot of back-woodsmen led by some cheap demagogues try to drive out error in a logical way they learn to their own intense surprise that what the world was thinking and doing had in some unaccountable way been kept from them. Either then, they have been deceived or are being attacked. They resent it and with the proper demagogue to lead they are ready to drive out heretics and defend the Truth as they have received it with gun and fagots.

Who is to blame? They that know; they that teach; they that have; they that sit silent and enjoy; great universities that close their doors to the mob; great scientists who prostitute truth to prejudice; great preachers who quibble with faith and facts; great rulers of wealth who fear understanding; and voluptuaries who have no wish to be disturbed by real democracy.

The folk who leave white Tennessee in blank and ridiculous ignorance of what science has taught the world since 1859 are the same ones who would leave black Tennessee and black America with just as little education as is consistent with fairly efficient labor and reasonable contentment; who rave over the 18th Amendment and are dumb over the 15th; who permit lynching and make bastardy legal in order to render their race “pure”. It is such folk who, when in sudden darkness they descry the awful faces of the Fanatic, the Fury and the Fool, try to hide the vision with gales of laughter.

But Dayton, Tennessee, is no laughing matter. It is menace and warning. It is a challenge to Religion, Science and Democracy.
