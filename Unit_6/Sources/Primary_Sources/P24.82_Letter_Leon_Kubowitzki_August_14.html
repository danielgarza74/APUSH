Letter to A. Leon Kubowitzki, August 14, 1944
John J. McCloy, Assistant Secretary of War · August 14, 1944
 
TOC FIT The Holocaust; The American Response
WHY HERE The War Department’s reply to a Jewish organization’s request to bomb the gas chambers and rail lines at Auschwitz gives the reasons it offered for refusing.
TREATMENT Public-domain text, complete (the letter it answers is summarized, not reproduced)
Historical Context

John J. McCloy, a Wall Street lawyer, served as assistant secretary of war from 1941 to 1945 under Secretary Henry L. Stimson. On August 9, 1944, A. Leon Kubowitzki, head of the Rescue Department of the World Jewish Congress in New York, wrote to McCloy forwarding a message that had reached him through the War Refugee Board from Ernest Frischer, a member of the Czechoslovak government-in-exile in London. Frischer urged the bombing of the gas chambers and crematoria at Oświęcim (Auschwitz) and of the railways leading there. McCloy answered five days later with this short letter. The text comes from a transcription of the original in the World Jewish Congress Records at the American Jewish Archives, published by Teaching American History.

Roosevelt had created the War Refugee Board in January 1944 to rescue victims of Nazi persecution. Between May and July 1944 the Germans deported more than 400,000 Hungarian Jews, most of them to Auschwitz, where the majority were killed on arrival. American bombers based in Italy could by then reach the area; on August 20 they attacked a synthetic-oil plant a few miles from the killing center. Read McCloy’s reasons one at a time. Historians still debate whether they were sound military judgments, and how much “study” lay behind them.

Editorial note. Only McCloy’s reply is reproduced. Kubowitzki’s letter of August 9, 1944, which the same web page prints, is a private unpublished letter whose author died in 1966, so it is summarized in the context instead. McCloy spells the name “Kubowitski”; the spelling is kept. The modern footnotes of the transcription are omitted.
Document Text

14 August 1944

Dear Mr. Kubowitski:

I refer to your letter of August 9 in which you request consideration of a proposal made by Mr. Ernest Frischer that certain installations and railroad centers be bombed.

The War Department had been approached by the War Refugee Board, which raised the question of the practicability of this suggestion. After a study it became apparent that such an operation could be executed only by the diversion of considerable air support essential to the success of our forces now engaged in decisive operations elsewhere and would in any case be of such doubtful efficacy that it would not warrant the use of our resources. There has been considerable opinion to the effect that such an effort, even if practicable, might provoke even more vindictive action by the Germans.

The War Department fully appreciates the humanitarian motives which promoted the suggested operation, but for the reasons stated above it has not been felt that it can or should be undertaken, at least at this time.

Sincerely,

John J. McCloy

Assistant Secretary of War
