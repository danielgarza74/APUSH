Primary Source P24.105
Letter to Samuel McCrea Cavert, August 11, 1945
Harry S. Truman · August 11, 1945
 
TOC FIT The Decision to Use the Atomic Bomb; Sowing the Whirlwind
WHY HERE Answering a church leader's protest two days after Nagasaki, Truman explains in a few blunt sentences why he believed Japan had to be bombed.
TREATMENT Public-domain letter, complete; the incoming telegram is described but not reproduced
Historical Context

Samuel McCrea Cavert was general secretary of the Federal Council of the Churches of Christ in America, a national association of Protestant denominations. On August 9, 1945, the day the second atomic bomb fell on Nagasaki, Cavert telegraphed the White House that many Christians were deeply disturbed by the use of atomic bombs against Japanese cities, because the weapon could not distinguish between targets and set a dangerous precedent for the future. He added that council leaders, among them Bishop G. Bromley Oxnam and John Foster Dulles, were preparing a statement urging that Japan be given time to learn the facts about the bomb and accept surrender terms before more cities were destroyed.

Truman answered on August 11, the day after Japan offered to surrender on condition that the emperor keep his throne. His reply is short and typed; the White House filed a copy with the telegram in the president’s Official File on the atomic bomb. Notice the reasons he gives: not invasion casualties or the length of the war, but Pearl Harbor, the killing of American prisoners of war, and the idea that bombing was the only “language” Japan understood. Compare the tone of this letter with his private diary at Potsdam, where he wrote that the bomb would be aimed at soldiers and sailors and “not women and children.”

Editorial note. Only Truman’s letter is reproduced. Cavert’s telegram of August 9, 1945 (two pages in the same NARA file) is a private communication whose author died in 1976; the Truman Library presumes copyright in such documents remains with the writers or their heirs, so it is summarized in the context instead. Handwritten White House filing notations on the letter are omitted.
Document Text

August 11, 1945

My dear Mr. Cavert:

I appreciated very much your telegram of August ninth.

Nobody is more disturbed over the use of Atomic bombs than I am but I was greatly disturbed over the unwarranted attack by — the Japanese on Pearl Harbor and their murder of our prisoners of war. The only language they seem to understand is the one we have been using to bombard them.

When you have to deal with a beast you have to treat him as a beast. It is most regrettable but nevertheless true.

Sincerely yours,

HARRY S. TRUMAN
