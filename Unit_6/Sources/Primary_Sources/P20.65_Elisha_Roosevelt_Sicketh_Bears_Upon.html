Elisha Roosevelt Sicketh the Bears upon the Bad Boys of Wall Street
Udo J. Keppler · May 8, 1907
 
TOC FIT Progressivism under Roosevelt and Taft: Taming Big Business; Roosevelt's Second Term
WHY HERE A 1907 cartoon shows Theodore Roosevelt, robed like a prophet, watching two bears labeled “Interstate Commerce Commission” and “Federal Courts” scatter a crowd of panicked Wall Street men.
TREATMENT Public-domain image
Historical Context

Udo J. Keppler drew this color cartoon for the centerfold of Puck, a New York humor weekly, in its issue of May 8, 1907. It shows President Theodore Roosevelt, not Franklin Roosevelt. The title borrows a story from the Bible (2 Kings 2), in which the prophet Elisha curses a crowd of youths who mock him and two bears come out of the woods and maul them. Here Roosevelt stands on a hill in a long blue robe, arms folded. Two huge bears, one labeled “Interstate Commerce Commission” and the other “Federal Courts,” charge into a crowd of robed men, many with labels on their clothes, who flee in every direction.

During his second term Roosevelt pressed for stronger federal regulation of railroads and large corporations. The Hepburn Act of 1906 gave the Interstate Commerce Commission power to set maximum railroad rates, and the Justice Department brought antitrust suits in the federal courts. Financiers blamed Roosevelt’s policies for falling stock prices in 1907. Notice how the cartoonist assigns the work of punishment to a commission and the courts rather than to Roosevelt himself, and consider whether the cartoon cheers or mocks the president.

Editorial note. Norton’s Online Reader places this Theodore Roosevelt cartoon among its images for the 1920–1945 period. The names lettered on the fleeing figures are too small to read reliably in the available scan and are not transcribed.
