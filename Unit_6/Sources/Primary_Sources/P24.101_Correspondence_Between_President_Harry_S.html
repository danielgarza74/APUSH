Primary Source P24.101
Correspondence Between President Harry S. Truman and Senator Richard Russell, August 1945
Richard B. Russell and Harry S. Truman · August 7–9, 1945
 
TOC FIT The Decision to Use the Atomic Bomb; Sowing the Whirlwind
WHY HERE A powerful senator urges the president to keep bombing Japan without mercy, and Truman answers that he will not wipe out whole populations unless it is absolutely necessary and expects Japan to give up once the Soviet Union enters the war.
TREATMENT Public-domain text, complete
Historical Context

Richard B. Russell of Georgia was one of the most influential Democrats in the Senate and an old colleague of Truman’s from his Senate years, which is why Truman’s reply opens “Dear Dick.” On August 7, 1945, the day after Hiroshima, Russell sent this telegram from his home in Winder, Georgia, to the White House. Truman was just arriving home from the Potsdam Conference, as the telegram’s closing notes; he answered on August 9, the day the second bomb fell on Nagasaki and the day after the Soviet Union declared war on Japan. Both documents survive in the White House’s Official File on Japan at the Truman Library. Russell names Joseph Grew, the former ambassador to Japan and a senior State Department official who had urged that Japan be allowed to keep its emperor, and he refers to the Bataan Death March of 1942.

Russell’s telegram shows what one strand of American opinion demanded in August 1945: unconditional surrender, removal of Emperor Hirohito, no warnings to Japanese cities, and more atomic, TNT, and fire bombing until Japan was “utterly destroyed.” Truman’s reply is worth reading line by line. He calls Japan cruel and uncivilized but refuses to act “in the same manner,” says he regrets “wiping out whole populations,” and predicts that Japan will “fold up” soon after the Russians enter the war. Ask what each man assumes about civilians, and what Truman’s last two sentences suggest about how he weighed American lives against Japanese ones.

Editorial note. Both documents are transcribed from the NARA PDF of the file. The telegram is in capital letters, as the Western Union operator typed it; Russell’s “JAPS” and “SHINTOLISM” (for Shintoism) and the misspelling “COMPTEMTUOUS” are reproduced as printed. The short headings are the editor’s. Telegraph routing codes, the White House receiving stamp, and handwritten filing notations are omitted.
Document Text
Telegram from Senator Richard B. Russell, August 7, 1945

WINDER GA AUG 7 427P

THE PRESIDENT

(PERSONAL DELIVERY) THE WHITE HOUSE

PERMIT ME TO RESPECTFULLY SUGGEST THAT WE CEASE OUR EFFORTS TO CAJOLE JAPAN INTO SURRENDERING IN ACCORDANCE WITH THE POTSDAM DECLARATION. LET US CARRY THE WAR TO THEM UNTIL THEY BEG US TO ACCEPT THE UNCONDITIONAL SURRENDER. THE FOUL ATTACK ON PEARL HARBOR BROUGHT US INTO WAR AND I AM UNABLE TO SEE ANY VALID REASON WHY WE SHOULD BE SO MUCH MORE CONSIDERATE AND LENIENT IN DEALING WITH JAPAN THAN WITH GERMANY. I EARNESTLY INSIST JAPAN SHOULD BE DEALT WITH AS HARSHLY AS GERMANY AND THAT SHE SHOULD NOT BE THE BENEFICIARY OF A SOFT PEACE. THE VAST MAJORITY OF THE AMERICAN PEOPLE, INCLUDING MANY SOUND THINKERS WHO HAVE INTIMATE KNOWLEDGE OF THE ORIENT, DO NOT AGREE WITH MR. GREW IN HIS ATTITUDE THAT THERE IS ANY THING SACROSANCT ABOUT HIROHITO. HE SHOULD GO. WE HAVE NO OBLIGATION TO SHINTOLISM. THE COMPTEMTUOUS ANSWER OF THE JAPS TO THE POTSDAM ULTIMATUM JUSTIFIES A REVISION OF THAT DOCUMENT AND STERNER PEACE TERMS.

IF WE DO NOT HAVE AVAILABLE A SUFFICIENT NUMBER OF ATOMIC BOMBS WITH WHICH TO FINISH THE JOB IMMEDIATELY, LET US CARRY ON WITH TNT AND FIRE BOMBS UNTIL WE CAN PRODUCE THEM.

I ALSO HOPE THAT YOU WILL ISSUE ORDERS FORBIDDING THE OFFICERS IN COMMAND OF OUR AIR FORCES FROM WARNING JAP CITIES THAT THEY WILL BE ATTACKED. THESE GENERALS DO NOT FLY OVER JAPAN AND THIS SHOWMANSHIP CAN ONLY RESULT IN THE UNNECESSARY LOSS OF MANY FINE BOYS IN OUR AIR FORCE AS WELL AS OUR HELPLESS PRISONERS IN THE HANDS OF THE JAPANESE, INCLUDING THE SURVIVORS OF THE MARCH OF DEATH ON BATAAN WHO ARE CERTAIN TO BE BROUGHT INTO THE CITIES THAT HAVE BEEN WARNED.

THIS WAS A TOTAL WAR AS LONG AS OUR ENEMIES HELD ALL OF THE CARDS. WHY SHOULD WE CHANGE THE RULES NOW, AFTER THE BLOOD, TREASURE AND ENTERPRISE OF THE AMERICAN PEOPLE HAVE GIVEN US THE UPPER HAND. OUR PEOPLE HAVE NOT FORGOTTEN THAT THE JAPANESE STRUCK US THE FIRST BLOW IN THIS WAR WITHOUT THE SLIGHTEST WARNING. THEY BELIEVE THAT WE SHOULD CONTINUE TO STRIKE THE JAPANESE UNTIL THEY ARE BROUGHT GROVELING TO THEIR KNEES. WE SHOULD CEASE OUR APPEALS TO JAPAN TO SUE FOR PEACE. THE NEXT PLEA FOR PEACE SHOULD COME FROM AN UTTERLY DESTROYED TOKYO. WELCOME BACK HOME. WITH ASSURANCES OF ESTEEM

RICHARD B RUSSELL US SENATOR.

Reply from President Truman, August 9, 1945

August 9, 1945

Dear Dick:

I read your telegram of August seventh with a lot of interest.

I know that Japan is a terribly cruel and uncivilized nation in warfare but I can’t bring myself to believe that, because they are beasts, we should ourselves act in the same manner.

For myself, I certainly regret the necessity of wiping out whole populations because of the “pigheadedness” of the leaders of a nation and, for your information, I am not going to do it unless it is absolutely necessary. It is my opinion that after the Russians enter into war the Japanese will very shortly fold up.

My object is to save as many American lives as possible but I also have a humane feeling for the women and children in Japan.

Sincerely yours,

HARRY S. TRUMAN
