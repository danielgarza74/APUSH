Primary Source P21.103
Explosion Rocks Wall Street
Butte Daily Bulletin (Butte, Montana) · September 16, 1920
 
TOC FIT The First Red Scare
WHY HERE A Montana labor newspaper’s front page on the day of the Wall Street bombing shows the confused first reports of the blast and accuses Attorney General Palmer of using it against organized labor.
TREATMENT Public-domain text, selected articles
Historical Context

The Butte Daily Bulletin was a labor daily published in Butte, Montana, a copper-mining city where the mines were dominated by the Anaconda Copper Mining Company and where strikes and union battles were frequent. The paper spoke for the city’s radical workers; its masthead promised to “preach the class struggle in the interests of the workers as a class.” This front page went to press on the afternoon of September 16, 1920, only hours after the explosion in New York, and it mixes wire reports from the United Press with a dispatch “Special to The Bulletin” and the paper’s own headlines. The page is preserved in the Library of Congress’s Chronicling America collection.

Just after noon that day, a horse-drawn wagon loaded with explosives blew up on Wall Street across from the bank of J. P. Morgan & Co., near the New York Stock Exchange. The final death toll was far higher than the sixteen reported here, and investigators came to believe it was a deliberate bombing, probably by anarchists; no one was ever convicted. The blast came after a year of mail bombs, strikes, and the Palmer Raids against radicals. Compare the reports on this page with one another: the accident theory, the plot theory, and the wild rumors. Then compare them with the headline the editors wrote over the Washington dispatch.

Editorial note. Only the articles about the explosion are reproduced; the rest of the front page (news of Italian factory seizures, local politics, and other items) is omitted. Headlines are given in the capitals and wording printed. The main report ends “(Continued on Page Two.)”; page two is not part of the saved file. The OCR text of this page is badly garbled in places, so the transcription was made from the page image.

EXPLOSION ROCKS WALL STREET
Sixteen Killed and 100 Injured When Truck of TNT Has Collision
Palmer and Red Baiters Attempt to Make Anti-Labor Capital Out of Explosion in New York Today

(Special United Press Wire.)

Washington, Sept. 16.—Private advices to a high government official said the New York explosion centered in the counting room of the J. P. Morgan company.

Department of Justice officials said all their reports indicate the explosion was the result of a plot. They declare they are unable to believe it due to a wagon load of explosives colliding with an automobile, which is the theory of the New York police.

Military intelligence bureaus are co-operating in the investigation, it was stated, indicating there may be an international angle to it.

DETROIT COPS ALSO NUTTY.

(Special United Press Wire.)

Detroit, Sept. 16.—The police commissioner assigned a squad of 50 detectives to guard Detroit’s financial district. Orders were given the detectives to search all cellars, and persons found loitering in the district. The action was taken following the explosion in the New York financial district.

AUTHORITIES DENY TALE THAT BOMB THROWN INTO OFFICES OF J. P. MORGAN

(Special United Press Wire.)

New York, Sept. 16.—Police reports indicated the explosion which shook the financial district today at noon, occurred in a wagon passing the Morgan company offices. One theory was that it was a load of TNT being taken to an excavation. The police advanced the theory that the wagon was struck by an automobile which was found in the wreck.

The police are unable to accurately estimate the number of the dead. Many wild rumors that more than 100 were killed flew about the financial district, but received no support in facts. The police believe, however, that more than 200 were injured or cut by flying glass.

(Special to The Bulletin.)

New York, Sept. 16.—Sixteen persons are known to have been killed by an explosion at Wall and Broad streets, in front of the offices of J. P. Morgan & Co. and the New York stock exchange today. The explosion occurred just at the lunch hour.

Over 100 pedestrians are reported injured.

The entire financial district was shaken. Windows in the Morgan building were shattered. Someone turned in a fire alarm and the fire apparatus and ambulances came dashing through the great throngs at the intersection of Wall and Broad streets, probably the most congested corner in the world at noon.

It was at first reported that a bomb had been exploded in an automobile standing at the curb in front of the Morgan offices. Another earlier report stated a bomb had been hurled into the Morgan offices, while a third report stated the missile had been thrown on the floor of the stock exchange building. These reports were later denied by the authorities, who claimed the blast had been caused by a collision of a truck loaded with TNT and another automobile at the street intersection.

The explosion was heard throughout the downtown section of New York. Huge masses of debris were thrown far into Wall and Broad streets. Dust from the explosion rose high in the air.

It was stated at the Morgan offices that no members of the firm were injured. J. Pierpont Morgan is said to be abroad. The hands of J. Spencer Morgan, son of Morgan, were cut by flying glass, it was learned.

Police lines were immediately drawn. Automobiles were commandeered by the police to carry the injured to hospitals.

The stock exchange was closed for the remainder of the day by the board of governors a few minutes after the explosion.
